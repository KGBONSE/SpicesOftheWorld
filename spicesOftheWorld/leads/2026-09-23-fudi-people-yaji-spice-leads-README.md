# Fudi People spice mix (yaji & co.) lead list — 2026-09-23

**File:** `2026-09-23-fudi-people-yaji-spice-leads.csv` (same folder).
**Built with:** the Clay connector plus plain web research, same hybrid method agreed for the
chilli oil list. Not published or sent anywhere — for Kofi's review only.

## Scope

Same core ICP as the chilli oil list (London/UK, independent-first, buyer-level contact), aimed
at who'd actually stock or serve a spice mix — restaurants that use it in cooking, and
grocers/wholesalers who'd sell the jar itself. I read this as covering the whole "yaji etc." family
of Fudi People's live dry blends (see the flag below on what's actually sellable today), not just
yaji, so Jamaican Jerk Rub-relevant Caribbean restaurants are in here too.

**22 companies, 24 contact rows**, in two groups:

- **Restaurants (11)** — suya/West African and Caribbean spots that would cook with yaji or Jerk
  Rub. Five of these (Chuku's, Enish, Akoko, Chishuru, Aso Rock, Chaconia, Umana Yana — 7,
  actually) are also on the chilli oil lead list; I'd pitch those once with both products rather
  than send two separate emails. Four are new: Alhaji Suya (a dedicated 3-site suya chain — about
  as direct a yaji customer as exists), Eko Wine Bar & Restaurant, 805 Restaurants (3 UK sites),
  and Deluxe Manna.
- **Grocers, cash & carries, and wholesale distributors (11)** — this is the new category you
  asked for. The standouts: **MP Foods** (35-year West African spice wholesaler, already sells
  the exact product category), **African Choice** and **Emmanuels Group** (both have real sales
  inboxes), and **St. Marcus Fine Foods** (South African specialty wholesaler already selling into
  delis/restaurants/farm stores — a good template for the kind of relationship you're after).
  **Wanis** and **Dees Imports** are the two biggest names on the list (Wanis is London's largest
  African/Caribbean cash & carry; Dees already supplies Tesco/Asda/Sainsbury's) — worth the extra
  effort even though neither has a direct email, just a contact form.

## Confidence key (same as the chilli oil list)

- **High** — named person + verified email, or a real, clearly-live sales/enquiries inbox.
- **Medium** — a general inbox confirmed on the business's own site, no named buyer.
- **Low** — real, verified business, but only a phone number, contact form, or (for 3 entries —
  Deluxe Manna, Nsawam Market, JEHOIAKIM Food Store) not even that yet; they're real listings from
  a London African/Caribbean shop directory but need a manual domain/contact lookup.

Clay's own database had almost nothing on these businesses (same gap as with the small African/
Caribbean restaurants on the chilli oil list) — every wholesaler and grocer contact here came from
their own websites, not Clay's contact search.

## Needs Kofi — read before sending anything

**Product readiness.** `docs/product-catalog-notes.md` confirms Yaji is one of the 20 dry,
shelf-stable blends — and `agents/marketing-team.md`'s shared rule says those 20 (plus chilli oil)
are the only products marketing should mention as for sale. But the same product-catalog doc also
notes the WooCommerce import is entirely **draft** right now: every blend still needs a price,
packaging size decided, and allergen labelling confirmed against real supplier ingredients before
it's actually orderable. Worth squaring that away — at least for Yaji and Jerk Rub — before any of
these leads get an actual pitch, so nobody's offered a jar that isn't really ready to ship yet.

**Wholesale vs. restaurant pitch.** The 11 grocers/wholesalers are a different sale than the
restaurants — they're buying to resell, not to cook with, so they'll want case pricing, MOQs, and
a wholesale price list, not a sample-and-see pitch. Worth having that ready before reaching out to
MP Foods, African Choice, Emmanuels, Ades, Dees, Wanis, or St. Marcus.

**Grosa Marketplace** is worth a second look separately from the rest — it's a B2B platform that
already aggregates a network of "accredited" African grocery merchants, so getting listed there
could reach many small shops through one relationship instead of outreach to each individually.

Three restaurant/grocer names (Deluxe Manna, Nsawam Market, JEHOIAKIM Food Store) are real but
still need a domain and contact found manually — didn't want to hold up the rest of the list for
three names.
