## 1. Core Voice Identity

Kofi is a farmer-founder tracing ingredient origins, not a detached food historian. The voice always answers "why do I personally care about this" before or alongside "what is this." History and science are in service of memory, not the other way around.

---

## 2. The Six-Part Structure (confirmed against source)

1. **Personal hook** — a specific, sensory childhood memory (usually Mokola Market, usually Mum)
2. **Geography/origin** — where the ingredient actually comes from, named specifically (place names, not "West Africa" alone)
3. **History** — how it moved through trade, empire, or colonization; often includes a "then it was forgotten/replaced" turn
4. **The blend** — literal ingredient list, given plainly, almost like a recipe card read aloud
5. **The dish** — what it's actually used for, day to day, on the street
6. **Throughline close** — returns to the personal hook, but extends it forward into present-tense action (the farm, the brand), not just nostalgia

## 3. Sentence-Level Patterns (with real examples)

**Comma-chained sensory triples/quadruples** — used to compress feeling into rhythm rather than explain it:
- "Sharp, peppery, warm"
- "for the smell, for the memory, for the market, for her"

**The "planted the seeds" motif** — not decorative, used literally and metaphorically at once. Reserve this specifically for origin-story moments; don't overuse it in every episode or it loses weight.

**Direct-address turn markers** — signal a shift in the story:
- "Here's the thing that gets me—"
- "Here's a tip if you're cooking with this at home—"

**Named specificity as authenticity** — lists of real, checkable details rather than generic description:
- "before the fish, before the plantain, before the Koobi"
This is the single most important thing to protect in future scripts — genuinity comes from specificity, not adjectives.

**Present-tense continuation close** — the episode doesn't end on a moral or summary. It ends by connecting the memory directly to what Kofi is doing right now:
- "now, on a farm in Sidcup, London, I'm growing my own chillies and spices, trying to carry that same handful forward"

## 4. What NOT to do (guardrails for Agent 3)

- Don't resolve the personal hook into a "lesson" — resolve it into present-tense action instead
- Don't invent specific memories/details not sourced from Kofi's real transcripts — flag with [NEEDS KOFI INPUT] instead of inventing
- Don't overuse "planted the seeds" — it's a signature phrase, not a filler transition
- Avoid generic sensory adjectives without specificity (e.g., "delicious," "amazing") — always anchor to place, person, or object

## 6. Second Register: "Demo Voice" (cooking-demo narration)

Source: jollof rice video transcript (auto-captioned — expect transcription noise/misheard words; patterns below are read from the *shape* of delivery, not exact wording).

This is a **distinct register** from Story Voice above. Use Story Voice for origin/history episodes; use Demo Voice for straight cooking-process videos.

- **Live, unscripted, in-the-moment** — narrates thinking as it happens, not pre-written ("let's have a look see how... it was very good, it was very good")
- **Frequent, casual direct address** — "if you want some of it," "don't worry about anything," "stay tuned" — more instructional/conversational than Story Voice's narrative address
- **Personal details dropped in passing, not built into an arc** — e.g. a self-mixed spice blend from Ghana, homemade dried prawns, home-grown salad — mentioned matter-of-factly, not structured as a memory callback
- **Repetition as live emphasis** — "really really good," "really really important" — doubled words in the moment, distinct from Story Voice's comma-chained triples
- **Stable sign-off habit** — subscribe reminder + "stay tuned," delivered consistently — worth using as a recurring outro template
- **Demonstrative/physical enthusiasm** — process is shown including hands-on mess ("tasted my hands"), not just described verbally

**Guardrail for Agent 3:** don't blend the two registers within one video — pick Story Voice or Demo Voice based on episode type, and stay consistent within that episode.

### Confirmed on second sample (fufu video) — patterns now seen twice, not one-offs

- **Opinion-first cold open** — starts with a strong, direct opinion/problem before any greeting or setup (e.g. distrust of shop-bought powdered/dyed products, "I don't eat them"), then pivots to "what I'm gonna show you is the best way" — this is a distinct hook style worth using deliberately for demo-episode openers
- **Family woven in naturally** — wife and child mentioned in passing as part of the cooking context, same low-key way the Ghana-trip spice blend was mentioned in the jollof video — confirms personal details are dropped in, not dramatized
- **Forward-tease of future content at the close** — explicitly previews upcoming videos (farm, vegetables) rather than just signing off — worth using as a recurring outro device alongside the subscribe reminder
- **Comparative framing against store-bought/inauthentic versions** — positions his method as better/simpler than both the shop version and even the "back home" version — this "better than both" framing is a persuasive device specific to Demo Voice

### Confirmed on third sample (salad/garden video) — now a 3-for-3 pattern, treat as core Demo Voice habits

- **Stated personal mission, spoken directly to camera** — "my main aim is to get people to grow their own stuff" — repeated twice in the same video, near-verbatim. This is the clearest sign yet of a real, reusable brand mission line — worth carrying into scripts explicitly, not just as an implied theme
- **Exact double subscribe close** — "don't forget to subscribe, don't forget to subscribe" said back-to-back, plus a closing "look after yourself, look after each other" — this trio (mission restated → double subscribe → well-wish sign-off) is now confirmed across multiple videos as the stable outro shape
- **Cross-promotion to his own other content mid-video** — references a separate dressing-recipe video by name while using the product, and previews upcoming farm/health videos at the close — confirms the forward-tease pattern from the fufu video is a habit, not a one-off
- **Enthusiasm expressed through repeated intensifiers, sometimes stacked 3-4 deep** — "really really really really good" — more extreme than the doubled words seen in the jollof video; treat this as a scalable device (can double or stack further for emphasis) rather than a fixed phrase
- **Ingredient tour is personal, not just instructional** — walks the garden narrating each plant with a small personal note (which ones he lets grow young vs. old, which he gives away) rather than just naming ingredients — this is Demo Voice's version of the specificity-as-authenticity marker seen in Story Voice, just applied to garden/produce rather than childhood memory

**Guardrail update:** the personal-mission line ("grow your own stuff," "encouraging people to grow") is now confirmed as a real, repeatable brand statement — Agent 3 can use it directly in garden/farm-adjacent episodes rather than treating it as one-off colour.

### Confirmed on fourth sample (roasted plantain/peanut video)

- **Sign-off phrase variant confirmed** — "take care of each other, take care of yourself" — a close cousin of the salad video's "look after yourself, look after each other." Treat this as one flexible sign-off template (care/wellbeing wish + subscribe reminder), not two separate phrases to memorize word-for-word
- **Casual, real-time experimentation is shown openly, including failures** — tries an ingredient substitution live ("something like this... actually came not good so I'm just gonna save this for later"), and openly walks through improvising with what's on hand (using cornmeal/grits instead of the usual garri) — this is a Demo Voice trait worth protecting: process is shown honestly, substitutions and misses included, not cleaned up into a perfect take
- **Practical substitution guidance folded into the demo itself** — names several possible alternatives for one ingredient (garri, cornmeal, polenta, couscous) as part of the narration, not as an aside — Demo Voice naturally includes flexible "here's what else works" guidance mid-process
- **A factual/health aside dropped in near the end, told with a little self-aware humor** — mentions a health benefit and immediately disclaims where he got it from ("I didn't say that, that's what it says") — a light, self-aware move distinct from Story Voice's straight-faced science asides

**Sign-off template (now confirmed across 3 of 4 Demo Voice samples):** mission/wellbeing wish → subscribe reminder (sometimes doubled) → "until next time" or equivalent close.
