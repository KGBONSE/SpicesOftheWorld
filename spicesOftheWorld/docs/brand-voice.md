# Fudi People — Brand Voice Profile

Working reference for the Script Writer agent and any manual drafting.
Built from three source samples: two narration recordings (Mokola1,
Mokola2 — farm/chilli-smoking footage, market-memory narration) plus one
independently-written memory passage. Patterns confirmed across all three
samples are treated as core, load-bearing signatures rather than one-off
tics — see `voice-recording-transcripts.md` for the raw source material.

**2026-07-26:** added a second source set — 6 raw farm voice memos,
transcribed locally (`faster-whisper`). These are a different kind of
recording (live vlog, not reflective narration), so they're analysed
separately below under "Farm vlog register" rather than folded into the
core signatures above, which stay specific to reflective narration.

## Core voice characteristics

- **Warm, memory-driven, and unhurried.** The narration doesn't state
  facts — it arrives at them through personal recollection. Even a simple
  geographic fact ("Mokola Market is in Accra, Ghana") gets routed through
  "growing up," "my favourite memories," "the trips I took with my mum."
- **Builds emotionally before it informs.** Sentences open with feeling or
  memory, widen into description, then land on a reflective or
  values-based close. This is a spiral structure, not a linear
  fact-delivery structure.
- **Reverent about food as inheritance, not just ingredient.** Spices
  aren't described as flavour agents first — they're sensory memory,
  family, place.

## Rhythm & sentence construction

- **Comma-chained clauses** rather than short declarative sentences —
  thoughts extend and layer.
- **Circling back for emphasis** — phrases like "as I said" reappear
  mid-thought, echoing spoken storytelling rather than scripted copy.
- **Trailing list-closes** — emotionally weighted passages end on an
  expanding list rather than a single tidy sentence (e.g. "...for spices,
  for farming, for beautiful food, for cooking, for organic food"). Core
  signature — don't tighten this into one word.
- **Light natural connectors** ("you know," "um," "uh") — thin these out
  in scripted form but don't eliminate entirely; roughly one per
  paragraph, never per sentence.

## Core stylistic signatures

- **Memory-first openers** — "Growing up in Ghana, my favourite
  memories..." is the default template for any origin-story or
  Africa-link segment, not just an occasional device.
- **Adjective stacking** — 2–3 emotionally-charged adjectives before a
  noun ("busiest, liveliest, most vibrant"). Vary the count naturally
  between two and three rather than defaulting to a fixed number.
- **Recurring metaphor — "planted the seeds for…"** — connects childhood
  memory to present-day passion (spices, farming, cooking). Strong enough
  to serve as a deliberate series motif — a recurring sign-off or section
  transition, not just an occasional callback.
- **References to his late mother** ("God bless her soul") — used
  naturally, unforced, mid-sentence. Handle with care: only where it
  arises organically from the memory being described, **never** inserted
  purely for emotional effect, and never in the geography/history/blend/
  dish segments unless there's a real, direct connection.
- Ghana/West Africa described through emotional and character qualities
  ("spirit," "resilience," "joy"), not just facts.
- **Concrete object/gesture anchor** — confirmed as the stronger hook
  variant on Episode 1 (2026-07-16): open on one specific physical
  detail (a stall, a seed cracked between fingers, a smell) rather than
  describing the scene/occasion in general terms. Mirror that same
  concrete image in the Africa Link close so hook and close echo each
  other — default template for every future episode's Personal Hook.

## Tone

| Trait | Notes |
|---|---|
| Pace | Unhurried, reflective — not punchy or hook-driven |
| Formality | Conversational, first-person, intimate |
| Emotional openness | High — grief, nostalgia, and joy sit comfortably side by side |
| Authority | Earned through lived experience and memory, not lecturing or "expert" framing |
| Humor | Not prominent — voice is sincere rather than comedic |

Rooted in specific place and memory (Mokola Market, Accra; the Sidcup
farm) rather than generic food-history narration. History and spice
science are always told through Kofi's own lived connection to them.

## Vocabulary signals

- Favour: "vibrant," "unforgettable," "liveliest," "joyful," "resilience,"
  "spirit," "amazing," "organic," "beautiful food"
- Avoid: culinary-industry jargon, corporate marketing language, short
  punchy ad-copy sentences — save punchy phrasing for on-screen text and
  thumbnails only, never for narration lines

## How to use this doc

When drafting or reviewing a script:
1. Check the opening line — does it start on memory/sense, not fact?
2. Check adjective density in descriptive passages (2–3, varied)
3. Look for a natural place to use the "planted the seeds for…" bridge if
   the passage is transitioning from past to present
4. On emotional beats, consider a trailing list-close rather than a tidy
   single sentence
5. If a reference to his mother appears, check it arose naturally from
   the memory rather than being inserted for effect

## Farm vlog register (a second, distinct register — added 2026-07-26)

Everything above was built from **reflective narration** — Kofi
recounting a memory after the fact, likely with some thought given
beforehand. The 6 raw voice memos transcribed 2026-07-26 (see
`voice-recording-transcripts.md`, Transcripts C–H) are a completely
different kind of recording: **live, present-tense farm-update vlogs**,
talking to camera while walking through the polytunnel or harvesting.
Same person, same underlying warmth, but a genuinely different register
— useful for anyone editing this raw farm footage, or writing narration
meant to sit *over* it.

- **Consistent opener**: "Hello, hello, Fudi people" — every single one of
  the 6 memos opens this way. A clear, repeatable greeting formula, distinct
  from the memory-first Hook opener used in scripted narration.
- **Self-dating, like a log entry**: several memos state the date and
  sometimes the time before anything else ("Today is the 7th of June,
  2026, and it's exactly 11:37 in the morning") — a documentary,
  keeping-a-record instinct.
- **Consistent sign-off**: "Look after yourself, look after each other"
  closes nearly every memo, sometimes followed by "bye," "cheers, bye,"
  or "speak to you soon." This register's equivalent of "planted the
  seeds for…" as a load-bearing, repeatable close — but a different
  phrase for a different context (vlog sign-off vs. reflective-narration
  bridge). Don't merge the two.
- **Present-tense, real-time description** — narrating what's in front
  of him as he sees it ("look at that," "oh my god," "wow"), not
  recollecting. Unscripted and unpolished by design.
- **Heavy repetition as rhythm/emphasis**, more pronounced than in the
  reflective register — doubling and tripling words or short phrases
  ("twist it, twist it, twist it," "really good, really good, really
  good," "we're doing okay, we're doing okay").
- **Visible thinking-out-loud** — mid-sentence self-corrections and word
  searches left in ("What do you call it? Oh, of course"), genuine
  uncertainty rather than polished delivery.
- **Real expertise surfaces unprompted** — practical growing knowledge
  (pot size vs. fruit size, twist-don't-cut harvesting technique, why
  smaller pots yield faster but smaller harvests) comes out naturally
  mid-ramble, not as a lecture. This is a good source of genuine,
  specific detail for future "the blend"/growing-process segments.
- **Family presence**: at least one memo has him working alongside a
  helper, giving casual real-time instruction. Warm, unforced — matches
  the brand's family-forward thumbnail photos.

**When to use which register:** the reflective register (rest of this
doc) for scripted narration — Hook, Close, voiceover sitting over
B-roll. This vlog register for anything using Kofi's actual farm-vlog
audio directly (e.g. if raw clips like the ones in `video/` ever get cut
with his real voice-over-shoulder audio rather than silent B-roll under
narration), or as a model for how an unscripted "in the moment" farm
segment should sound if one is ever wanted.
