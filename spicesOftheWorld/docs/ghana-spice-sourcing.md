## 1. Where to Source in Ghana

**Physical markets (smaller/mid-volume, negotiable pricing)**
- Kumasi Central Market — largest wholesale hub; typically cheapest for dried spices, closer to Ashanti/northern producers
- Techiman Market — major agricultural wholesale market, strong for goods from the north
- Makola Market, Accra — more retail-facing, convenient but pricier

**B2B export platforms (container-scale, MOQs often apply — commonly 20ft container minimums)**
- Tridge (dir.tridge.com) — supplier directory + market data for Ghana herbs/spices
- spiceexportersdirectory.com
- go4worldbusiness.com

## 2. UK Import Requirements

**Dried spices generally (ginger, grains of paradise, grains of selim)**
- No health certificate normally required to import herbs/spices into the UK
- UK law doesn't define specific legal standards for spices; HPA microbiological guidance is the relevant reference
- Standard food hygiene/labelling compliance still applies

**Fresh/unprocessed plant material (not applicable if fully dried before shipping)**
- Would require Phytosanitary Certificate + IPAFFS pre-notification
- Ghana-side authority for phytosanitary certificates: PPRSD (Plant Protection and Regulatory Services Directorate)

**Important flag found — does NOT apply to this sourcing plan:**
UK legislation (SI 2025/1162) lists Ghana-origin HS code 0904 (dried/crushed Capsicum/Pimenta — i.e. chillies/pepper) under increased border checks (50% physical inspection rate) for aflatoxins. Since chillies are excluded from this sourcing plan, this specific flag likely doesn't apply — but worth re-checking if the blend ever includes actual Capsicum/Piper pepper sourced from Ghana.

## 3. Commodity Codes (for duty/customs classification)

- **Ginger** — HS heading 0910 ("ginger, saffron, turmeric, thyme, bay leaves, curry and other spices"). Clean, well-defined code — lowest classification risk.
- **Grains of paradise** — botanically in the ginger family (Zingiberaceae); likely falls under 0910's "other spices" catch-all subheading. No dedicated named code found — some ambiguity.
- **Grains of selim** — botanically unrelated (Annonaceae) but functions as a pepper substitute; also likely falls under 0910's "other spices" catch-all. Same ambiguity as above.

**Recommended before shipping any commercial quantity:** get a binding classification ruling from HMRC or a freight forwarder for grains of paradise/selim specifically, since they lack a clean dedicated line and misclassification risks delays.

**Duty rates:** Ghana is not an LDC, so falls under DCTS's standard/Enhanced Preferences tier rather than the zero-duty LDC tier. Exact duty % not confirmed in this session — check trade-tariff.service.gov.uk directly per product, with Ghana as country of origin.

## 4. Supplier Vetting Checklist

**Drying & moisture control**
- Drying method (sun-dried on ground vs. raised mats vs. mechanical)
- Target moisture content before bagging (dried chillies typically <10-11%; ask equivalent for ginger/grains)
- Drying done off bare ground (reduces contamination risk)

**Storage**
- Storage conditions between drying and shipping (off ground, ventilated)
- Typical time in storage before shipping

**Sorting & quality**
- Do they sort out discoloured/damaged product before bagging?
- Can they supply a Certificate of Analysis (CoA) on request?

**Documentation & traceability**
- Can they provide a Phytosanitary Certificate from PPRSD if needed?
- Do they know the specific farm/region of origin for a batch? (Useful for compliance and for brand storytelling — origin tracing is core to the Fudi People story.)
- Prior UK/EU export experience?

**Commercial**
- MOQ and packaging options
- Who handles export logistics — them or Kofi?
