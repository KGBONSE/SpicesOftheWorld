# Ghana Spice Order List — Fudi People

Compiled by working directly through the 34 spice-blend rows (SKUs FP-01
through FP-34) in [`docs/woocommerce-product-import.csv`](./woocommerce-product-import.csv),
one per episode of the "Spices of the World" YouTube series. Every
`Description` field was parsed by hand to pull out only the **actual dried
spices, seeds, dried herbs, dried aromatics, and named dried chillies** —
fresh produce (onion, garlic, fresh ginger/chilli, fresh herbs), liquids and
condiments (oil, vinegar, wine, soy sauce, citrus juice, honey), plain sugar
and table salt, stock cubes, jaggery, and lentils were left out, since the
point of this list is what needs to be *sourced as a dried spice* from a
supplier, not the full recipe.

The result is **68 unique spice/dried-ingredient entries**, each tagged with
every episode/blend it appears in. Two tree nuts (almonds, hazelnuts) are
included because they're genuinely part of a blend (Dukkah) even though
they're not spices — flagged in the Notes column.

**On the photos:** every "Reference Photo" link below was found via live web
search (mostly Wikipedia/Wikimedia Commons, a couple of specialist retailer
or educational pages for the more obscure African items) and checked that it
actually loads and shows the ingredient. These are **visual references only**
— use them to sanity-check what a supplier sends, not as a substitute for
confirming the exact product, grade, and species with the supplier directly.
A few entries are marked "No reliable photo found" rather than guessing.

## Ghana-sourceable specialty spices

Most of the 68 items above (cumin, coriander, cinnamon, cloves, cardamom,
paprika, and so on) are globally traded spices available from more or less
any general spice wholesaler. The following, though, are the genuinely
**West/Central African specialty items** — the ones actually worth checking
first with a Ghana-based supplier specifically, since they may not be
reliably stocked elsewhere:

- **Grains of selim** (Ep.1 Yaji) — West Africa/Ghana
- **Grains of paradise** (Ep.7 Mbongo Mix) — West Africa
- **Calabash nutmeg** (Ep.4, Ep.7) — West Africa
- **Uda** (Ep.4 Pepper Soup Spice) — West Africa (possibly overlapping with grains of selim, see note above — worth clarifying with the supplier)
- **Njangsa** (Ep.7 Mbongo Mix) — Central Africa (Cameroon)
- **Mbongo spice** (Ep.7 Mbongo Mix) — Central Africa (Cameroon)
- **Korarima** (Ep.3 Niter Kibbeh) — Horn of Africa (Ethiopia) — worth asking about, though it's East African rather than West African, in case a Ghana supplier's network still carries it
- **Bobimbi** (Ep.7 Mbongo Mix) — Central Africa — the most obscure item on the whole list; no confirmed photo of the dried product was found (see table note), so this one especially needs an in-person or sample check with the supplier

Note: "Ashanti pepper" was named in the original research brief as a
possible West African specialty to look out for, but it does not actually
appear in any of the 34 blend descriptions in the CSV, so it isn't included
in the table above — worth asking the supplier about anyway if sourcing
regional specialties in bulk, but it's not something the current 34 blends
call for.

Everything else in the table — the cumins, cinnamons, cardamoms, dried
chillies, and so on — can most likely be sourced from any general spice
importer/wholesaler, in Ghana or otherwise, so treat the specialty list
above as the priority items to confirm with the Ghana supplier first.
