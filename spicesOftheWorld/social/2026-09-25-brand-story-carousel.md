## Caption (Instagram / Facebook)

Growing up in Ghana, one stall at Mokola Market always came first — before the fish, before the plantain. My mum would crack a seed between her fingers and let me smell it. That smell planted the seeds for everything after: for spices, for farming, for cooking, for Fudi People.

These days I grow the chillies myself, by hand, on a small farm in Sidcup, London. The first blend that came out of it was Yaji — the marinade behind Ghana's chinchinga and Nigeria's suya, same mix, two names. One stall has become six regions since, and every jar still traces back to that one smell.
