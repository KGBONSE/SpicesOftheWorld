# Black Cardamom

**Botanical name:** Amomum subulatum
**Taste profile:** Smoky, camphorous, resinous
**Also known as:** Winged cardamom, brown or greater cardamom, large cardamom, black gold
**Flavour group:** Penetrating Terpenes

## Origin & History
Long unfairly regarded in the West as an inferior substitute for green cardamom, black cardamom was mainly used for perfumes until the 20th century. It has traditionally been cultivated by the Lepcha community of Sikkim in northeast India, who use it medicinally but rarely in their own cooking — in China, by contrast, its culinary value has been appreciated for centuries. Cultivation outside Sikkim only began in the 1960s, spreading to Nepal, Bhutan, and eventually Darjeeling. Up to 90% of the world's black cardamom is still grown in Sikkim.

## Flavour Chemistry
Cineole dominates, the same compound found in green cardamom, but black cardamom's profile diverges from there with varied smoky phenols, clove-like eugenol, pine-like pinene, and citrusy limonene.

## Pairing Logic
- Allspice, cardamom, and galangal add complexity to the dominant cineole flavour.
- Nutmeg and cinnamon counter cineole's harsher edges with sweetness.
- Coriander and caraway bring out fruity notes via limonene.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
Best suited to slow-cooked, savoury dishes — Sichuan braised meats, Vietnamese pho and broths, and Indian/Nepali garam masala and pilafs. Most of the smoky flavour resides in the husk, so whole pods retain the meaty smokiness better than ground versions.

## Blends Featuring Black Cardamom
Garam masala, Shandong spice bag, Advieh, Vindaloo paste
