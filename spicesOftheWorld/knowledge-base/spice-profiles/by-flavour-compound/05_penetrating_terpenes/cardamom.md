# Cardamom (Green)

**Botanical name:** Elettaria cardamomum
**Taste profile:** Eucalyptus, citrusy, floral
**Also known as:** Small cardamom, green cardamom, true cardamom, queen of spices
**Flavour group:** Penetrating Terpenes

## Origin & History
Cardamom has served culinary and medicinal purposes in India for over 2,000 years, valued by Ancient Greeks and Romans as a perfume and digestive aid. Vikings reportedly encountered the spice during raids on Constantinople in the 9th century and carried it back to Scandinavia, where it remains popular in breads and pastries today. Cardamom began as a secondary crop on British Indian coffee plantations in the 19th century; introduced to Guatemala in 1914, that country is now the world's largest producer. Around 60% of all cardamom is consumed in Arab countries, where it's a key ingredient in gahwa, a fragrant coffee symbolizing hospitality.

## Flavour Chemistry
Cineole dominates with a powerfully penetrating eucalyptus character. Alpha-fenchyl acetate adds a sweet, minty, herbal note; limonene contributes citrus; linalool brings floral spiciness.

## Pairing Logic
- Black cardamom, bay, grains of Selim, allspice, and nutmeg combine sparingly with cardamom's eucalyptus profile.
- Galangal shares fenchyl acetate for aromatic depth.
- Fresh ginger boosts the lemony nuances.
- Coriander and lemongrass enhance the gentle floral qualities.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
Highly aromatic and versatile for both sweet and savoury dishes. Whole pods suit slow cooking; crushing pods before use releases oils from storage cells for a fresher, more intense flavour, ideal for quick-cooking dishes.

## Blends Featuring Cardamom
Turkish baharat, Advieh, Hawaij, Durban curry masala, Vindaloo paste, Shandong spice bag, Finnish gingerbread spice
