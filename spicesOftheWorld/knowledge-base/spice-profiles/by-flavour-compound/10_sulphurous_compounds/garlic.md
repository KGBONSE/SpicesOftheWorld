# Garlic

**Botanical name:** Allium sativum
**Taste profile:** Pungent, sulphurous, sweet
**Also known as:** Camphor of the poor, stinking rose
**Flavour group:** Sulphurous Compounds

## Origin & History
Garlic has been prized for over five millennia by virtually every civilization — the Greek physician Galen called it "the great panacea." An Egyptian medical papyrus from around 1550 BCE features 22 different garlic formulas, and the slaves who built the pyramids were given garlic to sustain them and prevent disease. Chinese cultivation valued its stimulating, healing properties, while Roman soldiers ate it before battle for courage and strength. Folklore has cast garlic both as protection from evil spirits and vampires and as a symbol of the Devil. It was shunned at Roman and Greek feasts for its odour, but over time became integral to many regional cuisines, including Indian and Mediterranean.

## Flavour Chemistry
Allicin, the compound most associated with "garlicky" flavour, gives garlic its sulphurous, spicy heat — it only forms when a clove is damaged, as chemicals react together. Smaller amounts of aromatic terpenes, including limonene and sabinene, are also present. Toasting creates additional nutty pyrazines.

## Pairing Logic
- Asafoetida and chilli add depth through similar sulphur compounds.
- Ginger and lemongrass team with the citrusy limonene notes.
- Black cardamom, nutmeg, and mace boost piney sabinene notes.
- Toasted sesame and wattle pair toasted garlic with other pyrazine-laden spices.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
Garlic amplifies and unites other flavours in savoury cooking, forming (with onion and ginger) the "trinity" at the heart of much Asian cuisine. Allicin levels peak roughly 60 seconds after crushing or chopping; uninjured cloves contain none. Vegetable oils spread the strongest compounds, while butter has a gentler effect. Avoid cooking above 180°C (350°F) to prevent bitterness. Sprouted cloves develop bitter defensive compounds — cutting out the green shoot reduces this bitterness.

Niter kibbeh, Mbongo mix, Nuoc cham, Shichimi-togarashi, BBQ rub

## Health Benefits *(not from the book — external research, added 2026-07-21)*
- **Heart health:** garlic modestly lowers blood pressure (especially in mild hypertension) and cholesterol, and increases fibrinolytic (clot-dissolving) activity.
- **Immune support:** aged garlic extract reduced cold/flu severity and missed work/school days in a clinical study.
- **Broad disease prevention:** antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and lipid-lowering effects studied across cardiovascular, metabolic and blood-sugar conditions.
- **Detox:** allicin has been shown to help reduce blood lead levels in at-risk workers.
- **Important caveat:** garlic's cancer-fighting claims remain inconclusive, and cold-prevention claims are based on weak evidence — avoid stating those as settled fact on camera. Also relevant: those on blood thinners should be cautious with high garlic intake.
