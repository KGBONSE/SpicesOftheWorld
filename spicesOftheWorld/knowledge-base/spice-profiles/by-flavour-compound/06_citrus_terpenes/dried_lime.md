# Dried Lime

**Botanical names:** Citrus x latifolia or C. aurantifolia
**Taste profile:** Tangy, sour, musky
**Also known as:** Persian lime, noomi (C. x latifolia); Omani lime, loomi (C. aurantifolia)
**Flavour group:** Citrus Terpenes

## Origin & History
Limes are native to Southeast Asia and were brought to the Middle East by Arab traders — exactly when is unclear, since lemons and limes were often referred to interchangeably in Arabic texts. By the 10th century CE, Arab traders had introduced limes to Egypt and North Africa, from where they spread across southern Europe during the Crusades era. Limes reached the Americas in the 16th century via European explorers planting them in the West Indies. The practice of drying limes originated in Oman, possibly discovered when unharvested fruit dried and fermented naturally on the trees. Dried limes are used throughout Middle Eastern and Indian subcontinent cooking, and are most closely associated with Persian cuisine.

## Flavour Chemistry
Citral and limonene provide the core citrus-herbal character. Linalool adds floral, spicy woodiness; methoxycoumarin contributes sweet, balsamic depth; fenchone brings bitter, camphorous warmth.

## Pairing Logic
- Ginger and cardamom add complementary citrus complexity.
- Nutmeg, allspice, and coriander emphasize similarly floral notes.
- Vanilla, mahleb, and fennel boost sweetness through shared coumarin.
- Cassia and grains of Selim work with the fenchone-driven camphor tang.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
Drying reduces lime's acidity (some acids break down into sugar), letting its woody, camphorous, floral notes shine more than fresh lime. Piercing whole dried limes with a skewer before cooking helps rehydrate the flesh and release flavour; because compounds dissolve into fats and alcohol, low-fat dishes made with dried lime tend to taste sweet and tart but lack aroma. Powder works best for quick-cook dishes.

## Health Benefits *(not from the book — external research, added 2026-07-21)*
- **Digestive:** traditionally used to ease diarrhoea and other gastric issues, and to stimulate appetite.
- **Immune support:** rich in vitamin C and antioxidants, supporting general immune function.
- **Heart health:** potassium content supports healthy blood pressure and heart muscle function.
- **Nutrient content:** also carries fibre, magnesium, folic acid, calcium and iron — a genuinely nutrient-dense flavouring, not just a sour note.
