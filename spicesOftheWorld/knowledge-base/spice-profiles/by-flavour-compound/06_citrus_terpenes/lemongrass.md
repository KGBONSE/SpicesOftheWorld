# Lemongrass

**Botanical name:** Cymbopogon citratus
**Taste profile:** Citrusy, peppery, refreshing
**Also known as:** Serai, oil grass, West Indian lemongrass
**Flavour group:** Citrus Terpenes

## Origin & History
The Latin genus name *Cymbopogon* derives from Greek words for "boat" and "beard," referring to the shape of its flowers — now rarely seen in cultivated varieties. Lemongrass has been used medicinally and as a flavouring across Asia for thousands of years; in 10th-century China it was placed on beds as an insect repellent. By the Middle Ages it had reached Europe via Asian caravan routes, appearing in period brewing and spiced wine recipes. India began cultivating it commercially in the 19th century, mainly to export the aromatic oil for soaps and cosmetics, though it was Southeast Asian rather than Indian cuisine that adopted lemongrass as a culinary staple.

## Flavour Chemistry
Citral makes up around 70% of lemongrass's flavour compounds. The key to pairing is avoiding doubling down on this dominant citrus note — instead, pair with spices that bring out its milder peppery myrcene or its sweet, floral linalool/geraniol side.

## Pairing Logic
- Coriander and ginger team well where citrus is a minor part of the profile.
- Black pepper and allspice boost the peppery myrcene notes.
- Cardamom, Sichuan pepper, and grains of Selim harmonize penetrating spices through shared floral compounds.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
Enlivens curries, stir-fries, pickles, salads, and soups. Most of its flavour comes from oil glands deep inside the grass, so slicing, grating, or pounding releases oils faster for quicker cooking, while gentle bruising suits longer cooking to avoid excessive evaporation. Cooking with fats like coconut milk helps spread the oil-based citrus and floral compounds through a dish.

## Health Benefits *(not from the book — external research, added 2026-07-21)*
- **Cardiovascular/metabolic:** lemongrass essential oils have shown cholesterol-lowering and glucose-control benefits in research.
- **Antioxidant/anti-inflammatory:** contains several antioxidants that scavenge free radicals, plus anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial activity shown in lab/animal studies.
- **Stomach protection:** in rat studies, lemongrass infusion reduced stomach lining damage from alcohol exposure.
- **Caveat:** despite its reputation as a calming tea, clinical trials in humans did NOT find lemongrass tea reduced anxiety — worth avoiding that specific claim on camera.
