# Amchoor

**Botanical name:** Mangifera indica
**Taste profile:** Citrus, herbaceous, bittersweet
**Also known as:** Mango powder, amchur
**Flavour group:** Sweet-Sour Acids

## Origin & History
Mangoes have been cultivated in India for more than 4,000 years and are deeply tied to Ayurvedic medicine and Hindu folklore — Ganesh, the elephant-headed god, is often depicted holding a ripe mango as a symbol of attainment. Buddhist monks carried mangoes to East Asia in the 4th–5th centuries CE, and the fruit travelled trade routes west, reaching Africa around 1000 CE and northern Europe by the early 14th century. Portuguese explorers introduced mangoes to Brazil in the 17th century, and they also reached Mexico via the Philippines; by the mid-to-late 18th century, mangoes had reached the West Indies, likely via Brazil.

## Flavour Chemistry
Ocimene dominates the flavour profile with floral, vegetal, tropical character. Cubebene adds lemony, radish-like notes; cadinene contributes herbal, woody spiciness.

## Pairing Logic
- Nigella, grains of Selim, annatto, and bay share ocimene-driven character.
- Sumac and coriander pair with the sharply fruity cubebene notes.
- Juniper, carob, and asafoetida enhance woody notes via cadinene.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
Fruity, very tart, slightly resinous with a citrus tang — used much like lemon as a souring agent, but without adding moisture. Drying reduces the mango's moisture from over 80% to under 10%, concentrating acids, sugars, and flavour compounds into a distinctly sweet-and-sour profile (fruit sugar concentration rises to around 65%). Add early for acidity and sweetness, or late for a more fragrant effect, since many terpene flavours evaporate quickly while the fruit sugars remain stable.

## Health Benefits *(not from the book — external research, added 2026-07-21)*
- **Digestive:** used in Ayurveda to ease acidity, aid digestion, and relieve constipation and flatulence.
- **Iron content:** a notable source of iron, traditionally valued for pregnant women and iron-deficiency anaemia.
- **Immune support:** good vitamin A and C content supports immune function.
- **Salt reduction bonus:** its bright tartness can stand in for some added salt, indirectly supporting healthy blood pressure.
- Direct clinical research on amchoor itself is limited — benefits are largely extrapolated from mango's known nutrient profile.
