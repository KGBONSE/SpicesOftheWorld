# Cumin

**Botanical name:** Cuminum cyminum
**Taste profile:** Earthy, herby, woody
**Also known as:** Roman caraway, jeera
**Flavour group:** Warming Terpenes

## Origin & History
Evidence of cumin use in Egyptian pyramids suggests it was in use more than 5,000 years ago. The Ancient Greeks and Romans used it as a table seasoning alongside salt — Pliny the Elder called it the "king of condiments" — and in modern Georgia and parts of Africa, salt combined with cumin remains a popular seasoning. From the 7th century CE, Arab traders carried cumin along spice caravan routes to North Africa and east to Iran, India, Indonesia, and China, embedding it in regional blends including baharat, garam masala, panch phoran, and ras el hanout. Spanish conquistadors introduced it to the Americas in the 16th century, notably to Mexico, where it became deeply woven into the cuisine.

## Flavour Chemistry
Cuminaldehyde is the source of cumin's distinctive musky, spicy flavour and is rarely found in other spices, though it does appear in roast beef and cinnamon. Pinene contributes a dry, pinewood note; cymene adds a fresh, turpentine-like edge.

## Pairing Logic
- Cinnamon, cardamom, and nutmeg pair with cumin's warming muskiness.
- Black pepper and black cardamom bring out the woody pinene side.
- Star anise, anise, coriander, ajwain, and nigella bridge with the citrus/turpentine cymene notes.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
An essential component of Indian, North African, Levantine, and Mexican cuisine. Cumin responds particularly well to toasting: bruising and dry-frying the seeds produces roasted pyrazine flavour compounds, some of which also carry sulphurous notes.

## Blends Featuring Cumin
Turkish baharat, Za'atar, Advieh, Dukkah, Hawaij, Chaat masala, Shandong spice bag, BBQ rub

## Health Benefits *(not from the book — external research, added 2026-07-21)*
- **Digestive relief:** cumin extract has significantly relieved IBS symptoms (bloating, pain, urgency) in early clinical studies, backing its long folk use for diarrhoea.
- **Blood sugar & cholesterol:** cumin powder in yogurt reduced LDL cholesterol and triglycerides while raising HDL in one study; other research links it to better glucose control.
- **Weight management:** several early studies suggest cumin can support weight loss as part of an overall healthy diet.
- **Antimicrobial:** lab studies show cumin limits growth of bacteria including E. coli.
