# Juniper

**Botanical name:** Juniperus communis
**Taste profile:** Resinous, sharp, floral
**Also known as:** Common juniper
**Flavour group:** Fragrant Terpenes

## Origin & History
Juniper is bound up with folklore of healing and magical protection — in one legend, the infant Jesus is hidden in a juniper hedge from King Herod's soldiers. Its branches have been burned since ancient times to smoke meat and fish, and to purify the air during plague outbreaks in the Middle Ages. Italy has exported home-grown juniper for over 500 years, most of it destined for gin distilleries — juniper remains a legally required dominant aromatic in all gin. The Dutch were reportedly the first to flavour spirits with juniper in the 13th century, producing a strongly alcoholic drink called jenever.

## Flavour Chemistry
Pinene dominates, giving a woody, spicy, camphorous character. Alpha-terpeneol, terpineol, and myrcene contribute herby, spicy, woody notes; limonene adds citrus; geraniol brings floral, lingering sweetness.

## Pairing Logic
- Cumin, grains of Selim, and black pepper combine with juniper's dominant pine notes.
- Bay and cardamom add clean herbal notes to strongly-flavoured terpenes.
- Lemon myrtle draws out the citrus side via shared limonene.
- Nutmeg and coriander highlight the fresh floral notes via geraniol.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
Juniper's clean, citrus qualities suit meat particularly well — its acids have a mild tenderizing effect. Berries don't need roasting but benefit from crushing or grinding to speed release of the terpene-rich oils, which spread easily through oil and alcohol but dissolve poorly in water.
