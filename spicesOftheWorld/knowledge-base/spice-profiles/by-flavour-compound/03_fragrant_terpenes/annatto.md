# Annatto

**Botanical name:** Bixa orellana
**Taste profile:** Peppery, earthy, sweet
**Also known as:** Achiote, bijol, lipstick tree, roucou, urucu
**Flavour group:** Fragrant/Warming Terpenes

## Origin & History
Sometimes called "poor man's saffron," annatto has been used as a natural dye for centuries. The Maya of Central America made a red body paint from the seeds for battle preparation, and the Aztecs used it as a ceremonial pigment and skin protectant, adding it to hot drinks to colour their mouths red — the source of the common name "lipstick tree." By the 17th century it had reached Europe and become a food colouring agent, notably for cheese and smoked fish, a use that continues today.

## Flavour Chemistry
Germacrene, elemene, and copaene — all part of a gently sweet terpene family — provide most of annatto's taste, while caryophyllene contributes a peppery, bitter contrast.

## Pairing Logic
- Juniper and asafoetida partner with the germacrene-driven sweetness.
- Coriander and sumac enhance citrus notes via elemene.
- Cinnamon, allspice, and clove draw out the underlying honey sweetness.
- Black pepper and grains of Selim reinforce the peppery caryophyllene side.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
Often used purely for its staining ability, but annatto also brings a peppery, slightly citrus, almost smoked-earth flavour. Whole seeds are hard and need time for flavour to escape, so cooking with oil or steeping in water works best. Annatto oil is stronger-tasting since most compounds dissolve better in fat; steeping in water requires more seeds since the water-soluble pigment (norbixin) is far less abundant than the oil-soluble one (bixin).

## Blends Featuring Annatto
Achiote paste (for marinades)

## Health Benefits *(not from the book — external research, added 2026-07-21)*
- **Antioxidant:** annatto's carotenoids (bixin, norbixin) neutralise free radicals and may protect against oxidative-stress-linked chronic disease.
- **Heart health:** early research suggests annatto may help lower blood pressure and LDL cholesterol.
- **Eye health:** its carotenoids help absorb harmful light and may guard against cataracts.
- **Antimicrobial/digestive:** shows activity against various pathogens; its fibre supports digestion.
- Culinary amounts are small (a pinch for colour), so treat health effects as a bonus, not the main reason to use it.
