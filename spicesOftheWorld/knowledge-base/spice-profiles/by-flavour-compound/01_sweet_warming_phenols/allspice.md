# Allspice

**Botanical name:** Pimenta dioica
**Taste profile:** Warm, sweet, peppery — cinnamon, clove and nutmeg in one berry
**Also known as:** Jamaica pepper, pimento, myrtle pepper, newspice
**Flavour group:** Sweet Warming Phenols

## Origin & History
Allspice is the dried unripe berry of a tree native to the Greater
Antilles and Central America — and it is the one major spice whose
commercial production has stayed entirely in the Western Hemisphere.
Spanish explorers met it in the Caribbean in the early 16th century and
carried it to Europe, where its resemblance to a blend of cinnamon,
clove, nutmeg and pepper earned it the English name "allspice" after
Britain took Jamaica in 1655. Repeated attempts to grow it commercially
elsewhere have never taken hold; Jamaica and Guatemala remain the
dominant producers. The Spanish name *pimienta* — "pepper" — is the same
naming confusion that stuck to chilli, another New World crop mistaken
for a pepper.

## Flavour Chemistry
Eugenol dominates — the same clove compound, here at high concentration,
which is why allspice reads as clove-forward. Beta-caryophyllene adds a
woody, drying, peppery edge (the same compound that gives black pepper
part of its character), and methyl eugenol, cineole (eucalyptus) and
smaller terpenes round it out. Berry and leaf oils differ: the leaf is
even more eugenol-heavy.

## Pairing Logic
- Clove, cinnamon and nutmeg reinforce the "three spices in one" impression through shared eugenol and cinnamaldehyde.
- Black pepper and bay draw out the caryophyllene woodiness.
- Chilli, ginger and thyme are the classic jerk partnership.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
The defining spice of Jamaican jerk, and widespread through Caribbean
stews, "pepperpot" and West Indian curry. It also runs through Levantine
and Turkish cooking (kofta, stuffed vegetables, some baharats), Northern
and Eastern European baking and pickling, and Scandinavian preserved
fish. Toast and grind whole berries just before use — pre-ground allspice
loses its top notes fast. Whole berries can be simmered into brines and
braises and removed.

## Blends Featuring Allspice
Jamaican jerk rub, Arabic baharat, Pickling spice, Mulling spice

## Health Benefits *(not from the book — external research, added 2026-09-05)*
- **Antioxidant:** in comparative testing allspice comes out with a higher antioxidant capacity than most common spices — driven by eugenol and its polyphenols.
- **Anti-inflammatory / pain relief:** eugenol has well-documented anti-inflammatory and topical analgesic action — the same reason it turns up in over-the-counter toothache remedies.
- **Early cancer-cell research:** allspice polyphenols, particularly ericifolin, have shown anti-proliferative effects on prostate cancer cells in lab studies — genuinely interesting, but strictly test-tube stage; not a claim to make on camera beyond "being studied".
- **Digestive:** traditionally used across the Caribbean and Middle East to settle the stomach, with some lab support.
- Evidence outside the eugenol/antioxidant basics is lab-only — keep spoken claims soft.
