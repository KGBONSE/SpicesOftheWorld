# Sesame 🌍

**Botanical name:** Sesamum indicum
**Taste profile:** Nutty, bittersweet, rich
**Also known as:** Benne
**Flavour group:** Toasty Pyrazines

## Origin & History — HIGH PRIORITY FOR FUDI PEOPLE
Sesame was first cultivated more than 4,000 years ago and prized by many ancient civilizations. **Sesame plants originated in sub-Saharan Africa** and remain cultivated in North Africa and Sudan today, alongside China, India, the Americas. Babylonians and Assyrians used it in cooking and religious rituals — Assyrian mythology holds that the gods drank sesame wine the night before creating the world. Ancient Egyptians used the oil medicinally and ground the seeds for flour, with seed remains found in Tutankhamun's tomb. Roman soldiers carried the seeds as emergency energy rations. In the Arabian folk tale, Ali Baba's "Open, sesame!" likely alludes to how ripe seed pods burst open at the slightest touch. Sesame travelled to North America and Mexico with the slave trade and was being grown in colonial America by 1730.

## Flavour Chemistry
Pyrazines and 2-furyl-methanthiol give roasted sesame its nutty, coffee-like, roasted-meat character. Furfural adds sweet, bready, almond notes; hexanal contributes green, grassy freshness.

## Pairing Logic
- Paprika and wattle emphasize the nutty, toasted flavours.
- Tamarind and vanilla intensify the bready furfural notes.
- Poppy seed and bay complement the green hexanal freshness.

## Culinary Uses & Technique
Used in everything from sweets (Indian til laddoos, Middle Eastern halva) to savoury dips like tahini and hummus; the Japanese sprinkle gomashio (sesame-and-salt) over rice and noodles. Untoasted seeds carry subtle flavour; toasting triggers a Maillard-type reaction between proteins and sugars, forming nutty pyrazines and sulphurous, roasted-meat compounds. Darker (black) seeds carry heavier, spicier aromas suited to savoury dishes; pale seeds work better for milder recipes and pastes.
