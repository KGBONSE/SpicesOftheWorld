# Chicken and Aubergine Biryani with Seven-Spice

A biryani inspired by the dish's Persian origins and its spread to India via Arab merchants. This version uses Lebanon's seven-spice blend (the region's answer to garam masala) for a rich, smoky, Middle Eastern character. Chilli flakes can be halved for a milder result.

**Serves 4–6 | Prep 30 mins | Cook 1hr 40–50 mins**

## Seven-Spice Blend
- 1 tbsp black peppercorns
- 1 tbsp allspice berries
- 1 tsp cloves
- 1 tsp coriander seeds
- 1 tbsp ground cinnamon
- 1 tsp ground ginger
- 1 tsp grated nutmeg

## For the Biryani
- 75g (2½oz) butter
- 4 skinless, boneless chicken thighs
- 4 cloves, 1 star anise, 8 black peppercorns
- 250g (9oz) basmati rice
- 4–5 tbsp vegetable oil
- 6–8 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- Sea salt flakes and black pepper
- 7cm (3in) fresh root ginger, finely grated
- 1 tbsp seven-spice (above)
- 1 tsp chilli flakes
- 2 aubergines, cut into 1cm cubes
- 100g (3½oz) frozen peas

## Method Summary
1. Grind the whole spices for the seven-spice blend and combine with the remaining spices; reserve 1 tbsp for the dish.
2. Sauté chicken thighs in butter with cloves, star anise, and peppercorns; cover with boiling water and simmer in a low oven (160°C) for about an hour until tender. Remove chicken, strain spices from the liquid.
3. Boil rice in the strained liquid for 7–8 minutes until softening. Shred the chicken meanwhile.
4. Fry garlic in oil, add ginger, chilli flakes, seven-spice, and salt, then the chicken and diced aubergine; stir-fry until coated.
5. Layer butter-tossed rice with the chicken/aubergine mixture in a lined pan, repeating layers and finishing with rice. Poke holes through for steam to rise.
6. Wrap the lid in a tea towel, cook on lowest heat for 35–45 minutes, adding peas 15 minutes before the end. Check for a golden crust (tahdig) on the bottom.
7. Flip onto a serving dish so the crispy rice base is on top.

## Spice Ideas from the Book
- Swap sweet spices for cumin or nigella for earthiness, or fenugreek for a musty, bittersweet note.
- Highlight cineole notes by swapping fresh ginger for cincole-rich galangal.
- Add fresh ginger later (with the peas) to preserve pungent gingerol/zingiberene notes.
