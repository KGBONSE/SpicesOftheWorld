## General Spice Science (background reference, applies everywhere)

- **What counts as a spice:** unlike herbs (always leafy), spices come from seeds, fruits, roots, stems, flowers, or bark — usually dried. Farrimond notes some strongly flavoured leaves (bay, curry leaf) get grouped as spices because they're used more like a background flavouring than a fresh addition.
- **Why spices taste of anything:** flavour compounds are chemicals the plant produces mainly for defence or reproduction — repelling pests, attracting pollinators — that happen to taste good to humans.
- **12 flavour groups:** Farrimond sorts every spice into one of 12 groups based on shared flavour compounds (e.g. Sweet Warming Phenols, Pungent Compounds, Sulphurous Compounds). Useful shorthand for the science segment: spices in the same group tend to blend well; spices with no shared compound are more likely to clash.
- **Key compounds worth naming on camera:** capsaicin (chilli's heat), piperine (black pepper's heat), eugenol (clove's warm medicinal note), cineole (cardamom/ginger's eucalyptus-like edge), anethole (star anise/fennel's liquorice note).

## 1. Horn of Africa (Ethiopia, Somalia, Eritrea) — "Fiery, Aromatic, Smoky"

- **Signature spices:** korarima (Ethiopian/false cardamom), fenugreek, chilli pepper, cardamom, cumin, coriander.
- **Supporting:** turmeric, ginger, garlic, nigella, clove.
- **Flavour identity:** Ethiopian cooking's defining taste comes from bittersweet fenugreek paired with smoky, floral korarima; Somali cuisine leans on the fruitiness of coriander, the cleanness of cardamom, and the earthiness of cumin.
- **Trade/history:** Addis Ababa is home to Africa's largest spice market. The region's signature *berbere* and milder *mitmita* blends are both dominated by red hot piri piri chillies. Saffron is a top Moroccan export but is rarely used locally — largely due to French colonial influence redirecting it abroad.
- **Local blend recipe — Niter Kibbeh (spiced clarified butter):** melt 500g unsalted butter with chopped onion, garlic, grated ginger, fenugreek seeds, ground cumin, korarima/cardamom seeds, turmeric, dried oregano, basil, and sage; simmer 20 minutes, then strain through muslin.
- **Africa-link note for other continents:** korarima's aromatic profile is often compared to true cardamom (South/Southeast Asia) — a natural bridge if you're later covering Indian or Middle Eastern cardamom.

## 2. The Maghreb (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya) — "Earthy, Rich, Mild"

- **Signature spices:** cumin, cinnamon, ginger, chilli.
- **Supporting:** coriander, turmeric, clove, paprika, allspice.
- **Flavour identity:** described as the portal where Africa meets the Middle East and Europe — spicing is sweet and mild overall, with cumin as the everyday workhorse and cinnamon bringing aromatic warmth to fruit-laden tagines.
- **Trade/history:** shaped by centuries of cultural exchange between indigenous Berbers and successive rulers — Arabs, Ottomans, French, and the period when Berbers themselves ruled much of the Iberian Peninsula as Al-Andalus. Arab traders introduced coriander, turmeric, and cloves from the east; paprika and allspice arrived later from the Americas.
- **Local blend recipe — Harissa:** soak 100g dried red chillies (Baklouti if available) in hot water 30 minutes, then blend with 3–4 garlic cloves, salt, lemon juice, ground cumin, ground coriander, ground caraway, and olive oil into a paste.
- **Africa-link note:** allspice and paprika's presence here is a direct thread to the Americas — a ready-made link if you're scripting a Central/South American episode later.

## 3. East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda) — "Zingy, Sweet, Fragrant"

- **Signature spices:** ginger, cardamom, garlic, cinnamon, clove.
- **Supporting:** bay, black pepper, cumin, coriander.
- **Flavour identity:** ginger gives East African cooking its characteristic piquancy and heat; cardamom, clove, and cinnamon add the sweet aromatic notes found in the region's version of chai.
- **Trade/history:** Zanzibar and the wider archipelago were the historic epicentre of East Africa's spice trade — over 2,000 years of trade with Arabia, Persia, and India introduced cloves, ginger, pepper, cinnamon, and cardamom. Ginger and cardamom travelled furthest inland; coastal cooking favours turmeric and coriander alongside coconut. Zanzibar's 1500s clove trade, established under Omani settlers, brought the island enormous wealth.
- **Local blend recipe — Pilau Masala:** toast and grind whole cumin seeds, black peppercorns, cloves, cardamom seeds, and cinnamon; stir into the pan when frying onions for pilau rice.
- **Africa-link note:** this is your strongest direct link to South Asia — the same trade routes that built the Zanzibar spice trade carried cardamom, clove, and pepper in from India, making East Africa a natural bridge episode between Africa and South Asia.

## 4. Central Africa (Cameroon, Nigeria's south, Angola, DRC region) — "Nutty, Pungent, Local"

- **Signature spices:** bobimbi (bark/seeds used as the region's garlic-equivalent), njangsa, mbongo.
- **Supporting:** calabash nutmeg, chilli (Scotch bonnet), ginger, grains of paradise.
- **Flavour identity:** with the exception of chillies (introduced by Portuguese explorers in the 15th century), Central African cuisine has stayed close to its indigenous spice cupboard. Three native pungent spices — Ashanti pepper, grains of Selim, and mbongo — provide lingering heat with hints of citrus.
- **Trade/history:** confusingly, "grains of paradise" is used locally as a name for four different spices (Ashanti, mbongo, grains of Selim, and true grains of paradise) — worth flagging on camera as a common point of confusion. Calabash nutmeg later reached the Caribbean via the European slave trade, where it became known as Jamaican nutmeg.
- **Local blend recipe — Mbongo Mix:** grind whole mbongo (or black cardamom) with bobimbi or wild/chive garlic, njangsa seeds (or unsalted peanuts), calabash nutmeg (or grated nutmeg), grains of paradise, and garlic cloves with chopped onion; used to flavour Cameroonian "black stew" (mbongo tchobi).
- **Africa-link note:** the calabash-nutmeg-to-Jamaican-nutmeg thread is a direct, ready-made link to a future Americas/Caribbean episode — and ties straight into the transatlantic slave trade history your channel can speak to directly.

## 5. West Africa (Ghana, Nigeria, Senegal, Francophone/Anglophone belt) — "Hot, Pungent, Smoky"

- **Signature spices:** chilli, ginger, garlic.
- **Supporting:** grains of Selim, grains of paradise, iru (fermented locust bean seeds), prekese, nutmeg, bay.
- **Flavour identity:** the region splits along colonial-era language lines — Francophone cooking favours mustard paste and souring agents like vinegar or lemon (e.g. Senegalese poulet yassa), while Anglophone cooking builds depth through smoked, fermented, and dried ingredients (dried/smoked crayfish, fermented locust beans).
- **Trade/history:** jollof rice originated in Senegal and has since become one of West Africa's most globally recognized dishes. Durban (Southern Africa) is noted as the largest Indian city outside India — relevant context if you're drawing a line from West Africa through to Southern Africa's Indian-influenced cooking.
- **Local blend recipe — Yaji:** combine grains of Selim, whole Ashanti pepper, crushed kuli-kuli (peanut puffs), ginger powder, cayenne pepper flakes, a crumbled stock cube, salt, and black pepper — the marinade spice mix for suya street-food kebabs.
- **This is your home territory** — Ghana sits in this region, so this is likely your richest source of direct personal-story material alongside Mokola Market footage.

## 6. Southern Africa (South Africa, Mozambique, Botswana, Zimbabwe) — "Hot, Sweet, Diverse"

- **Signature spices:** cinnamon, turmeric, coriander, chilli, garlic.
- **Secondary:** clove, black pepper, cumin, ginger, cardamom, bay.
- **Flavour identity:** South Africa's "Rainbow Nation" cuisine reflects its colonial history directly — Dutch colonization brought Indonesian-influenced spicing to Cape Malay dishes, while Indian indentured labourers under British rule made Durban a global contender for curry. Mozambique carries Portuguese-influenced piri piri chilli blends; Botswana and Zimbabwe use spice far more sparingly.
- **Trade/history:** this region shows some of the most direct, traceable colonial spice-trade lineage in the book — Dutch, British, Portuguese, and Indonesian/Indian influences all layered into one regional cuisine.
- **Local blend recipe — Durban Curry Masala:** toast and grind coriander seeds, cumin seeds, cardamom seeds, fenugreek seeds, cloves, a cinnamon stick, mild chilli powder, cayenne pepper, and ground ginger.
- **Africa-link note:** this sub-region is your clearest direct link into a future South Asia episode — the Durban curry tradition is literally Indian cooking transplanted via indentured labour, a strong on-camera trade/migration story beat (segment 2 of your framework).
