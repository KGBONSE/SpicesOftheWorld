## 2. Geography & Origin

The Andes stretch roughly 4,500 miles, from Argentina and Chile up
through Bolivia, Peru, and Ecuador to Colombia and Venezuela. At
1,000–3,000 metres and higher, slow-cooked stews and soups dominate,
chillies playing the lead role throughout. Ají amarillo, the yellow
chilli behind much of this region's heat, has been cultivated for more
than 8,000 years and was a key ingredient in Inca cuisine specifically.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Two regional salsas worth naming before the main blend: uchucuta, a
Peruvian salsa built from rocoto chilli and herbs, and llajua, its
Bolivian and Argentine equivalent — both proof that this "one sauce for
grilled meat" instinct existed here long before chimichurri got its name.
And one detail worth a genuine aside: molle, the dried pink berries of the
Peruvian peppertree, look and taste similar to true peppercorns but come
from an entirely unrelated plant — a reminder that "pepper" isn't always
what it claims to be, the same lesson Episode 1's grains of paradise
taught back at the very start of this series.

## 4. The Science

Coriander, the herb backbone of chimichurri, has shown a genuine
hypoglycemic effect in research, helping glucose tolerance, and separate
studies show it shifting cholesterol balance favourably — lower LDL,
higher HDL — while acting as a mild diuretic that may help lower blood
pressure. Its dominant compound, linalool, has even shown anxiety-reducing
effects in human studies. Most of the strongest evidence is still
animal-based, so any claim here stays in "may help" territory rather than
settled fact.

The rest of the sauce is quietly healthy too. The garlic goes in raw —
one of the most-studied ingredients there is for heart health and
immunity. Oregano is one of the more antioxidant-rich herbs by weight,
and the paprika brings carotenoids linked to heart and eye health. For a
condiment, chimichurri does a lot of good.

## 5. The Blend — Chimichurri

Combine 1 tsp chilli powder, ½ tsp dried chilli flakes, ½ tsp paprika, 2
finely chopped garlic cloves, 1 finely chopped onion, 4 tsp finely chopped
parsley or coriander, 1 tsp finely chopped oregano, 3 tsp white or red
wine vinegar, and 6 tbsp olive oil; season with salt and black pepper.

## Africa Link / Throughline Close

A herb sauce spooned onto meat straight off the fire — that's not an
Andean invention, it's the same basic move every farming culture makes
independently, Ghana included. Chimichurri just happens to be the version
that got famous. Same lesson as the spice bags a few episodes back: good
ideas get invented everywhere, on their own terms.
