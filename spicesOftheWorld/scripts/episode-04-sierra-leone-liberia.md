## 2. Geography & Origin

Sierra Leone and Liberia, sitting right along the coast, west of Ghana.
Liberia's story is a bit different from every other country we've covered
— it wasn't shaped by European colonial trade the same way. It was founded
in the 19th century by freed African-American settlers returning to West
Africa, so the food itself is a blend of indigenous West African cooking,
African-American influence, and Caribbean influence layered on top.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Remember jollof rice, from the Senegal episode? It didn't stop at Nigeria

— our spice from episode one, the one behind Yaji — shows up here again

## 4. The Science

Sierra Leone's pepper soup leans on that same grains-of-selim muskiness we
already know, but pairs it with something new here — calabash nutmeg,
which brings a bitter, almost medicinal edge to the broth. Between the
Scotch bonnet heat, the ginger, and that bitterness, pepper soup is built
to feel like it's doing you good while it warms you up.

And that "doing you good" feeling isn't just in your head — ginger's one
of the most genuinely well-evidenced spices going, especially for
digestion and nausea, and uda, the same grains of selim from episode one,
has real research behind it too, for gut and respiratory health. The
Scotch bonnet adds capsaicin's own file — heart health, circulation, a
small metabolic bump — and calabash nutmeg has been used as a medicine
across West Africa for about as long as it's been a spice.

## 5. The Blend — Pepper Soup Spice

Sierra Leone's pepper soup base is Scotch bonnet peppers, garlic, ginger,
country onion — a small, intensely flavoured wild onion — and often uda
and calabash nutmeg alongside it, all simmered together with goat, fish,
or chicken.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "PEPPER SOUP SPICE: Scotch bonnet + garlic + ginger + country onion + uda + calabash nutmeg"

## 6. The Dish — Dumboy

That same Scotch bonnet-ginger base turns up all over Sierra Leonean and
Liberian cooking, not just pepper soup — cassava leaf stew and groundnut
soup both lean on it too. But pepper soup's the one that pairs with what
we're making today.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Same base also flavours: cassava leaf stew · groundnut soup"

In Liberia, that same pepper soup gets served with dumboy — cassava,
boiled and pounded until it's smooth and stretchy, almost like a dough.
It's eaten communally, everyone pulling from the same bowl — it's less a
side dish, more a whole way of eating together.
