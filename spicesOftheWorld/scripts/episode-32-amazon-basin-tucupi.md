## 2. Geography & Origin

The Amazon is the world's largest rainforest, spanning nine countries,
principally Brazil, Peru, and Colombia. Its cuisine reflects ancestral
influence and genuine convergence between those three key countries, plus
European influence, chiefly Portuguese and Spanish. Amazon chillies rank
among the world's hottest — hundreds of native species, every shape and
size, typically used alongside garlic, cumin, and turmeric to marinate
meat and fish.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Two details worth knowing before we get to the sauce itself: jambú, a
flower nicknamed the "toothache plant" for its genuine numbing effect on
the mouth, flavours tacacá, one of Brazil's most popular soups — and it's
built on tucupí itself. Guaraná seeds, meanwhile, are used more as a
stimulant than a flavouring here. Both are proof that this region's
ingredients don't map neatly onto how the rest of the world uses spice —
Amazon cooking runs on its own logic.

## 4. The Science

Black pepper, tucupí's finishing spice, does more than add heat — its
piperine famously boosts absorption of other nutrients, including iron,
beta-carotene, and turmeric's curcumin, genuinely useful pairing knowledge
for anyone cooking with spice blends. Separate research shows piperine
improving blood lipid profiles — lower total cholesterol, LDL, and
triglycerides — in overweight or obese individuals, alongside real
antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects.

The garlic and chilli simmered into the sauce carry their own files —
garlic among the most-studied ingredients for heart health, and chilli's
capsaicin linked in large population studies to lower cardiovascular risk
and a small metabolic bump.

## 5. The Blend — Tucupí

Blend 2kg peeled sweet cassava root with 750ml water to a thick purée;
strain through muslin over a bowl to extract the starchy juice. Let
settle, then pour off the clear liquid, discarding the sediment. Cover and
leave at room temperature 24 hours to ferment. Combine with 2 finely
chopped garlic cloves, 3–4 finely chopped hot red chillies, and 1 tsp
ground black pepper; bring to the boil and simmer 30 minutes.

## 6. The Dish

Tucupí is described as a broth-like sauce served with duck, pork, or
fish — and it's the base of tacacá, the jambú-and-shrimp soup mentioned
earlier. But its single most famous pairing is pato no tucupí, Amazonian
duck simmered in this exact sauce until the meat is tender and the broth
carries all of it. That's today's dish.
