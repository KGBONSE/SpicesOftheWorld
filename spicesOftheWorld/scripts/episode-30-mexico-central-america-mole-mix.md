## 2. Geography & Origin

Mexico and Central America — a region with any of 60 different native
chilli varieties on hand, used fresh, dried whole, or powdered, depending
on the dish. Belizean cuisine draws on Maya, Garifuna, Creole, and British
influence; Mexican cooking carries debts to Mayan, Aztec, and
Spanish-colonial traditions layered on top of each other.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

The Maya used red and green jalapeños; the Aztecs used chipotle chillies,
cacao, and vanilla — all three genuine New World natives that spread
worldwide via the Spanish conquistadores. And here's the trade that built
today's blend specifically: those same conquistadores brought cumin,
black pepper, cloves, and cinnamon back the other way, straight into
Mexican kitchens, where they fused with native chilli and cacao to
produce something entirely new.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Aztec chilli + cacao meets Spanish cumin + cinnamon = mole"

## 4. The Science

Chilli's reach goes well past flavour — large population studies link
regular chilli and capsaicin consumption to lower cardiovascular disease
risk and lower all-cause mortality, and capsaicin itself can boost energy
expenditure and reduce appetite, genuinely useful alongside a healthy
diet. It's also shown real analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects, acting
directly on the body's TRPV1 pain receptors. Worth the balance on camera:
excessive intake can irritate the gut and worsen reflux.

And mole is unusually deep on this front for a sauce. The cacao nibs are
one of the most antioxidant-dense foods there is — more than green tea or
red wine. Sesame brings its cholesterol research, cinnamon its blood-sugar
research, and cloves one of the highest antioxidant loads of any spice.
Under all that richness, it's a genuinely nutrient-heavy mix.

## 5. The Blend — Mole Mix

Every country has its own version, always built on three different
chillies. Toast 2 each of dried ancho, pasilla, and mulato chillies with 3
tbsp cacao nibs, 1 tbsp sesame seeds, a 2cm cinnamon stick, ¼ tsp each
cumin seeds, anise seeds, whole black peppercorns, and cloves, plus ¼ tsp
each dried thyme and marjoram and 1 crumbled dried bay leaf. Grind, then
stir into fried onions and garlic and simmer with chopped tomatoes to make
a mole sauce.

## 6. The Dish

Every Mexican and Central American country has its own mole, and
tamales and cochinita pibil tell completely different stories from the
same region. But mole itself is the headline dish here — today we're
simmering the finished mole sauce with chicken, until it's thick, dark,
and coats every piece.
