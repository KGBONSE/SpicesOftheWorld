## 2. Geography & Origin

Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Goa — West India, a region that
genuinely spans two different climates in one: Rajasthan's dry, arid
heart against the tropical coastline running through Maharashtra and Goa.
Along the Konkan coast specifically, hot-and-sour fish curries get
balanced out with creamy coconut masalas, while Goa itself carries a
completely different, vinegary, garlicky signature — the direct result of
Portuguese colonisation.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Here's the real story: vindaloo comes from a Portuguese dish, carne de
vinha d'alhos — meat marinated in wine vinegar and garlic — that
Portuguese sailors and colonists brought to Goa in the 16th century. Goa
adopted it, swapped in local dried Kashmiri chillies for real heat, and
turned it into something entirely its own — fiery, sweet-sour, balanced
with jaggery. It's a direct, edible record of the same Portuguese trade
routes that carried spices through the Middle East into Europe — a thread
this series will pick back up properly once we get there.

## 4. The Science

Black pepper is one of the oldest, most heavily-traded spices in the
vindaloo paste, and it earns its place scientifically too — piperine, its
signature compound, genuinely boosts your body's absorption of other
nutrients, including iron and turmeric's own curcumin. That's not folklore,
it's real, well-established chemistry, and it's exactly why black pepper
and turmeric so often turn up paired together across this whole series.

## 5. The Blend — Vindaloo Paste

Toast 1½ tsp cumin seeds, 15 to 20 whole dried Kashmiri chillies, 1 tsp
black peppercorns, 2 tsp black mustard seeds, 1 tsp fenugreek seeds, 6
cloves, 1 tsp cardamom seeds, a 5cm piece of cassia, and half a star anise
in a dry pan until fragrant. Grind, then mix with 6 tbsp cider or palm
vinegar, 1 tsp salt, and 2 tsp jaggery or date palm sugar into a stiff
paste.

## 6. The Dish — Goan Vindaloo

Marinate chicken or pork, plus aubergine chunks, in the vindaloo paste
with garlic and ginger for at least an hour, ideally overnight. Fry onion
until soft and golden, add the meat, aubergine, and marinade, and brown
it off. Add tomatoes, chillies, and stock, bring to a simmer, then stir in
jaggery and vinegar until dissolved. Cover and cook low and slow until
tender and the sauce has thickened. Finish with coriander, and serve with
rice, yoghurt, and lime pickle.
