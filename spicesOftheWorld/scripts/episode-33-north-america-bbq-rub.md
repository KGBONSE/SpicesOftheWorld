## 2. Geography & Origin

North America — where European settlers originally brought only a
limited range of spices, mainly to preserve food for the long Atlantic
crossing. Gradually, spices from Central America (chilli, annatto), the
Caribbean (allspice), and South America (molle) left their mark on the
continent's cooking — though only chilli achieved lasting, widespread
popularity, helped along by Mexico's proximity.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Only chilli really stuck — thanks to Mexico next door"

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Today's North American cuisine is a genuine fusion — Mexican fajitas,
Indian curries, and Japanese ramen all sit comfortably in the everyday
diet, proof that "American food" was never really one thing. And BBQ rub
itself carries two rival regional identities in a single recipe: Kansas
City's love of brown sugar sweetness, and Memphis's preference for smoked
paprika over sweetness — genuine barbecue-belt rivalries, sitting
peacefully in the same bowl once you actually write the recipe down.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Kansas City sugar. Memphis smoke. Same rub."

## 4. The Science

Smoked paprika, the backbone of this rub, carries real carotenoids that
have lowered LDL and total cholesterol in a 12-week human study, plus
lutein and zeaxanthin linked to a lower risk of cataracts and
age-related macular degeneration. Its capsaicin content — higher in
hotter varieties — also brings genuine anti-inflammatory and pain-relief
properties, and may help improve insulin sensitivity.

## 5. The Blend — BBQ Rub

Mix 1 tbsp ground cumin, ½ tbsp cayenne pepper, 1 tbsp chilli powder, 3
tbsp brown sugar (a Kansas City touch), 1 tbsp ground black pepper, 2
tbsp smoked paprika (from the Memphis rub tradition), 1 tbsp onion
powder, 2 tbsp sea salt, and 1 tbsp garlic powder.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "BBQ RUB: cumin + cayenne + chilli powder + brown sugar + black pepper + smoked paprika + onion + salt + garlic powder"
