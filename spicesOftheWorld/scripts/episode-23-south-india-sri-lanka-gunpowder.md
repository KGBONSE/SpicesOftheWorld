## 2. Geography & Origin

Kerala's cardamom hills, Sri Lanka's cinnamon plantations, Andhra
Pradesh's renowned chilli harvest — the far south and Sri Lanka close out
South Asia the way this whole region opened, defined by what actually
grows there. Sri Lanka in particular, formerly Ceylon, was for centuries
the world's only known source of cinnamon — eight species grow there
natively, and all but one still grow wild today.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Sri Lanka — the world's only cinnamon source, for centuries"

## 3. Trade & Migration History

This coastline carries centuries of spice-trading influence — Portuguese,
French, Dutch, Southeast Asian, and British traders all left their mark,
adapted into something completely local. And here's a genuinely useful
thread to pull on before we leave South Asia: star anise, which turns up
in this region's cooking, almost certainly arrived via Chinese traders —
very little of it is actually grown here at all. That's not a loose end,
that's a door straight into where this series is heading next.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Star anise here — almost certainly Chinese traders, not local"

## 4. The Science

Tamarind is the sour backbone of South Indian cooking, and it's one of
the more broadly research-backed spices this whole series has covered —
genuine antioxidant power from its vitamin C and polyphenols, cholesterol
benefits shown in real studies, and even early evidence it can help
regulate blood sugar by slowing carbohydrate digestion.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Research backs tamarind for heart health and blood sugar, not just sourness"

## 5. The Blend — Gunpowder (Podi)

Dry-roast 25g chana dal and 25g split urad dal separately until coloured,
then toast 25g fresh or desiccated coconut. Add 1 tbsp black sesame
seeds, 2 tbsp curry leaves, and 6 to 8 red chillies, cooking until the
chillies darken. Stir in 1 tbsp tamarind pulp and ¼ tsp asafoetida, toast
for a minute, then add 2 tsp jaggery off the heat. Combine everything with
the lentils, cool, and grind to a coarse powder.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "GUNPOWDER: chana dal + urad dal + coconut + sesame + curry leaves + red chillies + tamarind + asafoetida + jaggery"

## 6. The Dish

Gunpowder is a condiment more than a dish in its own right — sprinkled
over rice, dosa, or idli, or mixed straight into oil as a dip. Today we're
keeping it simple and letting the powder do the work: a plain dosa, torn
and dipped straight into gunpowder mixed with a little sesame oil.
