## 2. Geography & Origin

Japan — a relatively small archipelago, but its spicing is surprisingly
diverse for its size. Central Japan is where perilla seeds, also called
shiso, are mainly grown — a plant in the mint family with a nutty, faintly
menthol-aniseed flavour unlike anything else on this table.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Perilla (shiso) — mint-family, nutty, faintly menthol"

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Nagasaki is the key to this whole episode — it became the Portuguese
traders' Japanese base in the late 16th century, and that single contact
point reshaped Japanese food more than almost anything before it. The
Portuguese brought deep-frying, chillies, and vinegar; the Chinese, coming
through separately, brought ramen, gyoza, and dumplings; the British later
brought curry. All of that sits alongside — not instead of — *washoku*,
the more subtle traditional Japanese cooking found everywhere else in the
country. It's the exact same Portuguese trading network that carried
chilli into Southeast Asia and paprika and allspice into the Maghreb —
Japan is simply the furthest-flung stop on that same route.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Nagasaki, late 1500s: the same Portuguese network that reached the Maghreb"

## 4. The Science

Sanshō, shichimi-togarashi's signature numbing spice, is a close cousin of
Sichuan pepper — same family, milder, more citrusy. Sichuan pepper itself
has over 140 identified antioxidant compounds capable of neutralising
free radicals linked to inflammation and ageing, and in a clinical study,
topical Sichuan pepper oil reduced knee osteoarthritis pain about as well
as standard anti-inflammatory drugs, with fewer side effects — a
genuinely well-studied result for a spice mostly known for its tingle.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Sichuan pepper oil vs. knee arthritis: on par with standard anti-inflammatories, in a real clinical trial"
**[B-ROLL]:** Close-up of sanshō or Sichuan pepper, grinding

## 5. The Blend — Shichimi-Togarashi

Mix together 2 tbsp finely ground sanshō (or Sichuan pepper), 2 tbsp dried
orange, yuzu, or lemon peel, 4 tbsp chilli powder, 2 tbsp nori seaweed
flakes, 2 tsp black sesame seeds, 2 tsp hemp/flax/chia seeds, and 2 tsp
garlic powder.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "SHICHIMI-TOGARASHI: sanshō + citrus peel + chilli + nori + black sesame + seeds + garlic powder"

## 6. The Dish

Shichimi-togarashi is mainly a table condiment — sprinkled over grilled
meats, curries, and noodle dishes rather than cooked into anything. Today
we're using it exactly that way: yakitori, skewered grilled chicken,
finished with a table sprinkle of shichimi right before serving.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "YAKITORI + SHICHIMI-TOGARASHI"
