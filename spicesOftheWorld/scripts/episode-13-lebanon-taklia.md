## 2. Geography & Origin

Lebanon's mezze culture is the Middle East's most recognisable culinary
export, even while dishes like hummus stay hotly disputed between Lebanon
and Israel next door. Lebanese cooks use spices liberally and
confidently, with a seven-spice baharat blend sitting at the centre of
mezzes, marinades, soups, and stews as a genuine kitchen staple.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Lebanon's seven-spice baharat is built from coriander, cinnamon, black
pepper, allspice, clove, ginger, and nutmeg — a genuinely different seven
from Turkey's baharat two episodes ago, proof that even the word "baharat"
itself keeps shifting shape as we move through this sub-arc. The
sweet-toothed Lebanese palate carries that same warmth into dessert too —
aniseed-spiced dishes like meghli rice pudding and sfouf tea cake lean on
the same warming spices as the savoury side of the kitchen.

## 4. The Science

Taklia's whole character comes down to one technique: frying garlic
gently in olive oil until fragrant but not coloured, which keeps its
flavour sweet and mellow rather than bitter. Ground coriander and a pinch
of cayenne go in after, off the heat, so their more delicate aromatic
compounds don't scorch. It's a small technique note, but it's the
difference between a finishing touch and a burnt one.

And for such a tiny blend, it's a genuinely healthy one. Frying the
garlic gently rather than hard keeps more of its heart-and-immunity
compounds intact — garlic is one of the most-studied spices there is on
that front. Coriander seed has real research behind blood sugar and
cholesterol, and even the pinch of cayenne pulls its weight for
metabolism and circulation.

## 5. The Blend — Taklia

Gently fry 3 sliced garlic cloves in 2 tbsp olive oil until fragrant, not
coloured. Grind with 1 tsp ground coriander, a pinch of cayenne, and ½ tsp
sea salt.
