## 2. Geography & Origin

Turkey sits genuinely at the crossroads of Europe, Asia, and the Middle
East, and it's been a spice-trading hub for centuries because of exactly
that position. The coastal, Aegean side of the country leans on fresh
herbs and lighter spicing; the eastern side carries heavier spicing and
sweetness, closer to what the rest of this sub-arc will sound like.

## 4. The Science

Turkish baharat leans on cardamom and nutmeg for its sweeter register
alongside the more familiar cumin and coriander — but the real signature
touch is dried mint stirred in right at the end, which most of this
series's blends haven't used yet. It's a genuinely different way of
finishing a spice mix: fresh and herbaceous instead of another layer of
warmth.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Dried mint — the finishing touch most blends don't use"
**[B-ROLL]:** Close-up of dried mint, whole spices toasting

## 5. The Blend — Turkish Baharat

Grind 2 tbsp black peppercorns, 2 tbsp cumin seeds, 2 tbsp coriander
seeds, 1 tsp cloves, ½ tbsp cardamom seeds, and 1 tbsp ground nutmeg, with
a pinch of ground cinnamon. Mix in 1 tbsp dried mint. Makes roughly 9 tbsp.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "TURKISH BAHARAT: black pepper + cumin + coriander + cloves + cardamom + nutmeg + cinnamon + dried mint"

## 6. The Dish

Turkish baharat is used in meat kofte, pilafs, and roast vegetables —
aubergine especially. Today we're making kofte: seasoned minced meat,
shaped and grilled, the baharat doing all the real work.
