## 2. Geography & Origin

From the Gulf of Panama to the tip of Patagonia, this vast Pacific
coastline draws heavily on seafood — fried, grilled, or stewed. Ají limo,
a hot chilli with a strong lemony flavour, takes its name from the Lima
region specifically; alongside ají panca and ají chombo, it anchors this
coast's whole approach to heat: fresh, sharp, citrus-forward.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Indigenous culture, Spanish colonization, the slave trade, and Asian and
Arab migrations have all left their mark on this coastline, from delicate
chilli-and-coconut fish soups to hearty charquicán, a beef and vegetable
stew. But the detail worth closing this whole series on is the Nikkei
tradition — a genuine fusion of Japanese and Peruvian cooking, drawing on
ginger, sesame, tamarind, and umeboshi pickled-plum paste. That's not a
loose thread. It's the exact same East Asian spice cabinet this series
just spent six episodes inside, showing up again on the opposite side of
the Pacific.

## 4. The Science

Garlic anchors leche de tigre, and it's one of the most well-studied
spices in this entire knowledge base — it modestly lowers blood pressure
and cholesterol, and increases the body's clot-dissolving activity.
Clinical research on aged garlic extract has also shown it reducing
cold/flu severity and missed work or school days. Worth flagging on
camera: garlic's cancer-fighting claims remain inconclusive, and anyone on
blood thinners should be cautious with high intake.

And it's in good company. The fresh ginger alongside it is about the
best-backed spice going — clinically proven for nausea, with real
blood-sugar research on top. The aji chilli brings capsaicin's
cardiovascular and metabolic evidence, and the raw lime juice the whole
thing is built on is straight vitamin C. Marinade the fish and drink the
leftover leche de tigre, as Peru does, and you're getting most of that.

## 5. The Blend — Leche de Tigre

Combine ½–1 finely chopped ají limo (or habanero), 1 crushed garlic
clove, 2.5cm coarsely grated ginger, 1 tbsp finely chopped fresh coriander
stalks, ½ finely chopped small red onion, and the juice of 5 limes; season
with salt; refrigerate 1 hour.

## 6. The Dish

Leche de tigre is the marinating liquid for ceviche, traditionally drunk
on its own after the fish itself has been eaten — genuinely worth doing
on camera exactly that way. Elsewhere on this coast, Nikkei-style tiradito
(thin-sliced raw fish in a Japanese-influenced dressing) tells the same
fusion story from a different angle. But today we're making the dish
leche de tigre was built for: fresh white fish, cubed and marinated
directly in it until the flesh turns opaque, then served with the extra
leche de tigre on the side, to drink.
