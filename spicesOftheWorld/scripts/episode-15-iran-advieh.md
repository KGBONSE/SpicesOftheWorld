## 2. Geography & Origin

Iran sits at the historic centre of the Persian Empire and the Silk Road
itself, invaded over centuries by Greeks, Arabs, Turks, Mongols, and
Uzbeks — and its cuisine carries every one of those crossroads. Iran
alone produces over 90% of the entire world's saffron, the single most
expensive spice that exists, used exclusively as whole burnt-red strands
here, never as powder.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Iran — 90%+ of the world's saffron"

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Here's a thread worth carrying forward, alongside Turkey's Sichuan pepper
a few episodes back: the "Road to China," an overland route active since
114 BCE, was the conduit that carried East Asian spices like ginger and
star anise into Turkish and Persian cooking — and rose petals specifically
were first brought into this region by Chinese traders travelling that
exact route. Two separate threads now, both pointing the same direction:
East Asia, later in this series.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "The 'Road to China' — active since 114 BCE"
**[B-ROLL]:** Trade route map animation

## 4. The Science

Saffron's flavour compound, safranal, gives it that honeyed, hay-like
character, and it shares real chemistry with cinnamon and nutmeg — sweet
spices with hints of honey, harmonising through the same compound. That's
exactly why advieh pairs rose, cardamom, and cumin against cinnamon and
ginger the way it does — the sweetness isn't accidental, it's built on
shared chemistry.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Saffron and cinnamon share real chemistry, not just 'both sweet'"
**[B-ROLL]:** Close-up of saffron strands, rose petals

## 5. The Blend — Advieh

Grind 2 tbsp dried rose petals, 2 tbsp cardamom seeds, and 1 tbsp cumin
seeds. Combine with 2 tbsp ground cinnamon and 2 tbsp ground ginger.

## 6. The Dish

Advieh is sprinkled over savoury rice, rubbed onto meats, stirred into
stews, or used in Persian rice pudding — genuinely both a savoury and
sweet-side spice. Today we're making a saffron-advieh rice pilaf, the two
signature Iranian ingredients in one pot: saffron for colour and
fragrance, advieh stirred through for warmth.
