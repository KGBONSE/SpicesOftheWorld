## 2. Geography & Origin

Iraq's spice story goes back to ancient Mesopotamia itself — this is one
of the oldest continuously spiced cuisines on earth. Persian traders added
fresh, fruity spices over the centuries; Silk Road and Spice Route
merchants layered in sweet-and-sour Chinese cassia alongside cardamom,
turmeric, and fenugreek arriving from South Asia.

## 4. The Science

Dried lime — sour, musky, genuinely unlike fresh citrus — is what defines
Iraq's favourite grilled-carp dish alongside turmeric, and it's a more
nutrient-dense spice than its size suggests: real vitamin C, potassium for
heart health, plus fibre, magnesium, and iron. Traditionally it's also
used to ease digestive complaints, which modern research broadly backs up.

The baharat blend itself stacks up well too. Allspice and clove both run
on eugenol — one of the most antioxidant-dense compounds in any spice,
and the reason clove has been a toothache remedy for centuries. Cinnamon
brings its blood-sugar research, coriander and cumin their gut and
metabolic benefits, and the black pepper helps your body absorb more of
all of it.

## 5. The Blend — Arabic Baharat

Grind 1 tbsp black peppercorns, 1 tbsp allspice, 1 tsp cloves, 1 tsp
coriander seeds, and 1 tsp cumin seeds. Combine with 1 tbsp cinnamon and 1
tsp grated nutmeg.

## 6. The Dish — Masgouf

Sour flavours — tamarind, turmeric — define Iraq's favourite fish dish,
masgouf: whole carp, split and grilled, traditionally over an open wood
fire. Rub the fish with Arabic baharat, turmeric, and a little tamarind,
then grill until the skin crisps and the flesh flakes.
