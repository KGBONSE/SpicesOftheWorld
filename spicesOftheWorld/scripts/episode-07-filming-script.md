### 2 · Geography & Origin
Cameroon, southern Nigeria, Angola, the DRC region — Central Africa. This
is the one region in the whole series that's stayed closest to its own
indigenous spice cupboard. The signature spices here — bobimbi, njangsa,
mbongo — you won't find profiled in most spice books at all. Chilli's the
one exception, introduced by Portuguese explorers in the 15th century, same
story as episode one.

*On-screen: "Bobimbi, njangsa, mbongo — spices you won't find in most spice
books" · B-roll: map graphic*

### 3 · Trade & Migration History
*(This is the heavy one. Slow down.)*

Here's something worth knowing before you cook with any of this — locally,
"grains of paradise" is used as a name for four completely different
spices: Ashanti pepper, mbongo, grains of Selim, and true grains of
paradise. Easy to get confused, so I want to be clear about that upfront.
And then there's calabash nutmeg — this one later reached the Caribbean
through the European slave trade, where it became known as Jamaican nutmeg.
I'm not going to soften that. That's the actual route this spice took.

*On-screen: "Calabash nutmeg → the Caribbean, via the slave trade →
'Jamaican nutmeg'" · B-roll: historical map, handled with restraint — no
upbeat music here*

### 4 · The Science
Calabash nutmeg is a different plant from the "true" nutmeg most spice
racks carry, but it plays a similar role in cooking. True nutmeg's own
chemistry is a good reference point — myristicin gives it that woody aroma,
eugenol and cineole add clove-like, eucalyptus notes. There's even a
compound in there, discovered fairly recently, that triggers a numbing,
cooling sensation on your tongue — even though nutmeg doesn't taste cold at
all.

True nutmeg's got real antioxidant and antibacterial research behind it too
— though it comes with a genuine caution: a little truly goes a long way,
because too much nutmeg is actually harmful, not just overpowering. The
rest of the mix pulls its weight, though: grains of paradise — the same
West African spice from episode one — has real research on revving up
metabolism, black cardamom brings antioxidant and respiratory benefits,
and the garlic ground through is one of the most-studied ingredients there
is for the heart.

*On-screen: "Nutmeg's numbing sensation — a real compound, not just heat" /
"Go easy on the nutmeg" / "Grains of paradise: metabolism · Black cardamom:
antioxidants · Garlic: heart" · B-roll: close-up of grating nutmeg/calabash

## Session 2 — Kitchen
*Grinding the mix. Camera close on the grinder and the texture.*

### 5 · The Blend — Mbongo Mix
Grind whole mbongo — or black cardamom if you can't get it — with bobimbi
or wild garlic, njangsa seeds or unsalted peanuts, calabash nutmeg or
grated regular nutmeg, grains of paradise, and garlic cloves with chopped
onion.

*On-screen: "MBONGO MIX: mbongo + bobimbi + njangsa + calabash nutmeg +
grains of paradise + garlic + onion" · B-roll: grinding spices, close-up
texture shots*

### 6 · The Dish — Mbongo Tchobi
*(Can be voiced over the footage.)*

Njangsa on its own — one of the seeds in this mix — is also what gives
ndolé, Cameroon's national dish, its bitter-greens depth. Today, though,
we're staying with what the full mix was actually built for.

This mix flavours mbongo tchobi — Cameroon's "black stew." Rich, dark,
built entirely around spices most people watching this have never tasted.

*On-screen: "Njangsa also builds: ndolé (Cameroon's national dish)" then
"MBONGO TCHOBI — Cameroon's 'black stew'" · B-roll: stew simmering, finished

### Africa Link / Throughline Close
*(Pairs with quiet, restrained Sidcup farm footage — no upbeat close)*

Those two bags are still on my counter. One name, four spices, was a funny
mix-up at the start of this episode. What I found next wasn't funny at all.
I want to sit with this one instead of rushing past it. Calabash nutmeg
travelling to the Caribbean wasn't trade in the way ginger or cumin
travelled — it went there through the slave trade, carried by force, not
choice. That's part of Africa's spice story too, and I'm not going to skip
it just because it's heavier than the rest of this series. It's exactly why
telling these stories properly matters to me — not just the flavour, the
whole truth of how it got here.
