## 2. Geography & Origin

Saudi Arabia, Yemen, Oman — the Arabian Peninsula, sharing the same
cumin-coriander-cinnamon trinity as its northern neighbours, but adding
dried limes and saffron carried over from Iran. As the peninsula
stretches toward Yemen and Oman, with Africa and Asia both within reach
beyond it, the flavours intensify — this is genuinely the most heavily
spiced corner of the whole Middle East sub-arc.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Here's the parallel worth sitting with: hot chillies arrive here from
Africa, masala mixes, turmeric, and coconut arrive from Asia — and that's
almost exactly the same Indian Ocean trade story the East Africa episode
told, just approached from the opposite coastline. Zanzibar and Oman were
trading across the same ocean, the same centuries, in both directions at
once.

## 4. The Science

Hawaij's backbone is cumin and black pepper in far greater quantity than
most blends this series has covered — a real, assertive dose rather than
a background note — balanced against turmeric's own earthy bitterness.
The sweeter variant strips out the pepper and cumin entirely in favour of
ginger, cloves, cinnamon, and fennel, proof that one name can cover two
genuinely different spice personalities depending on whether it's headed
into a stew or a cup of coffee.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "One name. Two completely different blends."
**[B-ROLL]:** Close-up of cumin and turmeric, whole spices

## 5. The Blend — Hawaij

Grind 1 tbsp black peppercorns, 7 tsp cumin seeds, 1 tbsp cardamom seeds,
and 1 tbsp coriander seeds. Combine with 2 tbsp ground turmeric.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "HAWAIJ: black pepper + cumin + cardamom + coriander + turmeric"
**[B-ROLL]:** Grinding the whole spices, the finished blend
