## 2. Geography & Origin

North India — Punjab, Delhi, the old Mughal heartland. This is where
garam masala actually comes from, and it's built for a climate of extreme
seasonal swings, hence the name: "garam" means warming. Further south,
Kerala grows roughly three-quarters of all of India's cardamom, and the
Malabar coast, right along that same southern stretch, was for centuries
the main export point for the peppercorns that built the entire global
spice trade — Portugal was the first European power to get rich off it,
back in the 16th century.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Kerala — ~75% of India's cardamom, today"

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Here's the thread worth pulling on. Right back in episode one, I told you
the Portuguese found a direct sea route to Asia, and the world moved on
from grains of paradise to "real" black pepper. This is where that real
pepper actually came from — Kerala's Malabar coast. Same story, different
side of it. And curry powder itself has its own colonial story: the
British in India encountered dozens of completely distinct regional spice
blends — garam masala in the north, panch phoran in the east, chaat
masala in the centre — and instead of learning them, they flattened all
of it into one packaged, standardised "curry powder," shipped it home to
Britain, and sold an entire empire's worth of spice tradition back to
itself as a single jar. It's a genuinely different thing from anything an
Indian kitchen actually makes.

## 4. The Science

Garam masala leans on black cardamom for its penetrating, smoky depth —
a different plant entirely from the green cardamom most people know, and
one of the more expensive spices in the world gram for gram. Nutmeg adds
a woody warmth from myristicin, cassia brings the same warming
cinnamaldehyde punch as true cinnamon, and cloves round it out with the
highest eugenol content of any spice going. Toast whole spices before
grinding and you pull out roasted, nutty pyrazine notes you don't get
from pre-ground powder — same rule as every blend this series has covered.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Black cardamom — a different plant, one of the world's priciest spices"
**[B-ROLL]:** Close-up of black cardamom pods, whole spices toasting

## 5. The Blend — Garam Masala

Grind 25g black cardamom seeds, 25g cassia broken into pieces, 25g black
peppercorns, 10g black cumin seeds, 2 blades of mace, 10g cloves, and an
eighth of a whole nutmeg, chopped, to a fine powder, then sieve out any
fibres.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "GARAM MASALA: black cardamom + cassia + black peppercorns + black cumin + mace + cloves + nutmeg"
**[B-ROLL]:** Toasting and grinding the whole spices, sieving the powder

*(Note: exact quantities straight from the source — no test-cook needed
for ratios. Black cumin/shahi jeera and mace can be harder to source
outside specialist shops — worth flagging on screen or substituting
regular cumin/extra nutmeg if needed.)*

## 6. The Dish — Chana Masala with Sweet Potato and Spinach

Garam masala doesn't stop at one dish — it finishes roadside kebabs, gets
stirred into hearty dhals, and shows up in Bhopal's biryanis alongside a
heavy hand of ground coriander. But today we're using it the way most
North Indian home cooks actually do — as the finishing spice, stirred in
right at the end.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Garam masala also finishes: kebabs · dhals · biryani"

Fry cumin seeds until aromatic, soften onion, add garlic and ginger, then
ground coriander, paprika, and turmeric. Add sweet potato, coat well, then
tomatoes and chickpeas plus chilli, and simmer until the potato's tender.
Stir in spinach until wilted, then finish with the garam masala itself
right at the end and a squeeze of lemon. Serve with naan.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "CHANA MASALA — sweet potato, spinach, finished with garam masala"
**[B-ROLL]:** Curry simmering, spinach wilting in, garam masala stirred in
at the end, finished plate with naan
