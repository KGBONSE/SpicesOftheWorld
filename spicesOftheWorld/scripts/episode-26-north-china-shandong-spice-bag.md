## 2. Geography & Origin

Shandong, Henan, and Beijing — a vast stretch of northern China that, for
all its size, shares a more integrated spicing style than the country's
other regions. Shandong cuisine is regarded as the primary tradition among
China's eight major regional cuisines, built on gentle spices that
preserve rather than dominate the main ingredient. Henan leans more
delicate but harmonious; Beijing draws from both, with a real emphasis on
the sour edge of the dark vinegar used throughout the region.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Cumin and chilli, the two most assertive spices in this region's cooking,
didn't originate here at all — they came in through the kitchens of
Chinese Muslims, and Beijing's own famous *yangrou chuan*, lamb skewers
coated in cumin and chilli, is the clearest proof of that history still
sitting on a grill today. It's the same Silk Road story that carried
cumin west into the Middle East centuries earlier — the Han Chinese
capital, Xi'an, marked the actual starting point of that road, a
6,400km network that stayed the world's greatest trade thoroughfare until
faster sea routes took over in the 15th century.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Yangrou chuan: Beijing's lamb skewers, seasoned via Chinese Muslim trade"
**[B-ROLL]:** Trade route map animation, the Silk Road's Xi'an starting point

## 4. The Science

Cumin is one of the more broadly evidenced spices in this whole series —
cumin powder stirred into yogurt has reduced LDL cholesterol and
triglycerides while raising HDL in real studies, and separate research
links it to better blood sugar control. Early clinical work has also shown
cumin extract meaningfully relieving IBS symptoms like bloating and pain.

## 5. The Blend — Shandong Spice Bag

Combine 10 cardamom pods, 2 star anise, ½ tbsp whole Sichuan pepper, ½
tbsp cloves, ½ tbsp ground cumin, a 7.5cm cinnamon stick, a 10–12cm piece
of liquorice root, and dried orange or tangerine peel in a muslin bag; tie
and drop into the cooking water.

## 6. The Dish

The spice bag's real job is infusing the master stock for northern
Chinese chicken stews, Dezhou- or Daokou-style — and alongside it, this
same region gives us those cumin-and-chilli lamb skewers, and simple
wheat dumplings, jiaozi, boiled in plain water. Today we're going straight
to what the spice bag itself is built for: a Dezhou-style master-stock
chicken, the whole bird simmered low in a stock carrying everything from
that bag until the meat falls from the bone.
