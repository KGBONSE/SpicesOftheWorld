## 2. Geography & Origin

Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra — Central India, a region shaped as much
by its neighbours as by itself. It's a part of the country that leans
hard into contrast: sweet against sour, nutty against tart, softened
vegetables given a real pickled edge from a simple trio of fried spices.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Central India — built on contrast, not comfort"
**[B-ROLL]:** Map graphic, Central India imagery if available

## 3. Trade & Migration History

The signature move here is a trio of fried nigella, fenugreek, and
fennel seeds — nothing exotic, just three seeds most of this series has
already met, combined in a way that gives softened vegetables and meaty
masalas a genuine pickled sharpness. Further east, the same instinct
shows up as panch phoran, literally "five spices" in Bengali, and even
that has its own regional variation — versions further east swap in a
local seed called radhuni in place of mustard. Same idea, region by
region, never quite the same twice.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Panch phoran = 'five spices' — never quite the same twice"
**[B-ROLL]:** Trade route map animation, whole spices frying in oil

## 4. The Science

Amchoor — dried mango powder — is what gives chaat masala its sour punch,
alongside asafoetida for a savoury, almost umami depth that Indian
vegetarian cooking leans on the way other cuisines lean on garlic and
onion. Asafoetida in particular is one of the better-studied spices for
digestion — research backs it as a genuine bloating and cramping aid, not
just an old home remedy.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Research shows asafoetida is a genuine digestive aid, not just a home remedy"
**[B-ROLL]:** Close-up of amchoor powder and asafoetida, dry-roasting spices

## 5. The Blend — Chaat Masala

Dry-roast 2 tbsp cumin seeds, 1 tsp coriander seeds, and 1 tbsp black
peppercorns until fragrant, then cool. Mix with 3 tbsp amchoor, 1 tsp
ground ginger, ½ tsp asafoetida, 1 tbsp kala namak — that's Himalayan
black salt — and 1 tsp salt, then grind to a powder.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "CHAAT MASALA: cumin + coriander + black pepper + amchoor + ginger + asafoetida + kala namak + salt"

## 6. The Dish

Chaat masala turns up sprinkled over roasted peanuts, stirred through
yoghurt, and dusted onto grilled kebabs — it's less a single dish's
seasoning, more the all-purpose finish of Central Indian street food. But
its most famous home is chaat itself — the whole category of savoury
snacks it's named after.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Chaat masala also finishes: roasted peanuts · yoghurt · kebabs"

Today we're building a simple fruit and potato chaat — boiled potato,
chickpeas, cucumber, a squeeze of lime, and a generous dusting of the
masala right at the end. Sharp, salty, sour, all in one bite.

## Africa Link / Throughline Close

What gets me about chaat masala is how unglamorous its ingredients
actually are — dried mango, a resin that smells strange straight out of
the jar, a salt that smells faintly of eggs — and yet put together it's
completely addictive. That's the same lesson every regional blend in this
whole series keeps teaching me, Ghana included: it was never really about
rare or exotic ingredients, it's about what a place does with what it
actually has.
