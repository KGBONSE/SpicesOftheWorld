## 2. Geography & Origin

Cameroon, southern Nigeria, Angola, the DRC region — Central Africa. This
is the one region in the whole series that's stayed closest to its own
indigenous spice cupboard. The signature spices here — bobimbi, njangsa,
mbongo — you won't find profiled in most spice books at all. Chilli's the
one exception, introduced by Portuguese explorers in the 15th century,
same story as episode one.

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Here's something worth knowing before you cook with any of this — locally,
"grains of paradise" is used as a name for four completely different
spices: Ashanti pepper, mbongo, grains of Selim, and true grains of
paradise. Easy to get confused, so I want to be clear about that upfront.
And then there's calabash nutmeg — this one later reached the Caribbean
through the European slave trade, where it became known as Jamaican
nutmeg. I'm not going to soften that. That's the actual route this spice
took.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Calabash nutmeg → the Caribbean, via the slave trade → 'Jamaican nutmeg'"

## 4. The Science

Calabash nutmeg is a different plant from the "true" nutmeg most spice
racks carry, but it plays a similar role in cooking. True nutmeg's own
chemistry is a good reference point — myristicin gives it that woody
aroma, eugenol and cineole add clove-like, eucalyptus notes. There's even
a compound in there, discovered fairly recently, that triggers a numbing,
cooling sensation on your tongue — even though nutmeg doesn't taste cold
at all.

True nutmeg's got real antioxidant and antibacterial research behind it
too — though it comes with a genuine caution: a little truly goes a long
way, because too much nutmeg is actually harmful, not just overpowering.
The rest of the mix pulls its weight, though: grains of paradise — the
same West African spice from episode one — has real research on revving up
metabolism, black cardamom brings antioxidant and respiratory benefits,
and the garlic ground through is one of the most-studied ingredients there
is for the heart.

## 5. The Blend — Mbongo Mix

Grind whole mbongo — or black cardamom if you can't get it — with bobimbi
or wild garlic, njangsa seeds or unsalted peanuts, calabash nutmeg or
grated regular nutmeg, grains of paradise, and garlic cloves with chopped
onion.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "MBONGO MIX: mbongo + bobimbi + njangsa + calabash nutmeg + grains of paradise + garlic + onion"

## 6. The Dish

Njangsa on its own — one of the seeds in this mix — is also what gives
ndolé, Cameroon's national dish, its bitter-greens depth. Today, though,
we're staying with what the full mix was actually built for.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Njangsa also builds: ndolé (Cameroon's national dish)"

This mix flavours mbongo tchobi — Cameroon's "black stew." Rich, dark,
built entirely around spices most people watching this have never
