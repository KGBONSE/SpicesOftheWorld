## 2. Geography & Origin

Guangdong — Cantonese cooking — is the most southerly of China's eight
great food traditions, and it's the one that's travelled furthest:
extensive emigration from this coastal region carried techniques like
steaming and stir-frying worldwide. Hunan, Fujian, and Taiwan sit
alongside it, leaning on more intense flavours through drying and
preserving rather than Cantonese cooking's simpler condiment style.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Cantonese cooking: the most-travelled of China's 8 great cuisines"
**[B-ROLL]:** Map graphic, South Chinese/Cantonese market imagery if available

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Cantonese food's simplicity is the point — light soy sauce, garlic,
ginger, sesame oil, nothing shouting over the main ingredient — and that
simplicity is exactly what made it portable enough to spread with
emigration into Chinatowns worldwide. Taiwan tells a slightly different
story: white pepper paired with pork belly is a local signature, and
shajiang, sand ginger, carries the same fresh heat as regular ginger but
with a mildly medicinal edge found nowhere else on this table.

## 4. The Science

Fennel seed, one of five-spice's core ingredients, is best known for
settling digestion — its fibre and volatile compounds relax intestinal
muscle, genuinely easing gas and bloating, and it's the spice's
best-supported traditional use by far. Small studies also show fennel
seed intake lowering total cholesterol, LDL, and fasting glucose, though
the human trials so far are small — worth treating as promising rather
than proven on camera.

And it's not carrying the health case alone. Cinnamon has its blood-sugar
research; cloves one of the highest antioxidant loads of any spice; star
anise is the natural source of shikimic acid, the compound the antiviral
Tamiflu is built from; and Sichuan pepper brings its own antioxidant and
anti-inflammatory file. Five spices, five research files.

## 5. The Blend — Five-Spice Powder

Grind 2 star anise, 1 tsp fennel seeds, 1 tsp cloves, a 5cm cinnamon
stick, and 1 tbsp whole Sichuan pepper to a fine powder, sieving out
fibres if needed.

## 6. The Dish

Five-spice rubs roasting meats and seasons stir-fries and stews right
across southern Chinese and diaspora cooking — Cantonese crispy roast pork
and Hunan's more intensely spiced braises both lean on it. But its most
famous single dish is Taiwanese: lu rou fan, braised minced pork rice,
built almost entirely around five-spice's warmth. Today that's the one
we're making — pork belly, minced and slow-braised in soy, rice wine, and
five-spice until it's rich and sticky, spooned over steamed rice.
