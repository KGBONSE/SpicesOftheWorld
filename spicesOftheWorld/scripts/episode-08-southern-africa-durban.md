## 2. Geography & Origin

South Africa, Mozambique, Botswana, Zimbabwe — Southern Africa. South
Africa calls itself the "Rainbow Nation," and nowhere is that truer than
in the food. Dutch colonisation brought Indonesian-influenced spicing
into Cape Malay cooking; Indian indentured labourers under British rule
turned Durban into a genuine global contender for curry.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Durban — a global contender for curry"
**[B-ROLL]:** Map graphic

## 3. Trade & Migration History

This region has some of the most direct, traceable colonial spice-trade
history we've covered — Dutch, British, Portuguese, and Indonesian/Indian
influence, all layered into one cuisine. Mozambique carries Portuguese-
influenced piri piri; Botswana and Zimbabwe use spice far more sparingly
than their neighbours. But Durban is the real story here — its curry
tradition isn't inspired by India, it's literally Indian cooking,
transplanted through indentured labour and never left.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Durban curry — literally Indian cooking, transplanted"
**[B-ROLL]:** Historical trade route map animation

## 4. The Science

Coriander is the spice tying this one together, and it's ancient — an
8,000-year-old stockpile was found in a cave in Israel. Its flavour comes
mostly from linalool, that lilac-floral note. And here's a nice callback —
coriander bridges with cumin, the spice from our Maghreb episode, through
a shared compound called cymene. Different regions, same chemistry.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Coriander + cumin share real chemistry — cymene"
**[B-ROLL]:** Close-up of coriander seeds, toasting

## 5. The Blend — Durban Curry Masala

Toast and grind coriander seeds, cumin seeds, cardamom seeds, fenugreek
seeds, cloves, a cinnamon stick, mild chilli powder, and cayenne pepper,
with ground ginger stirred through.
