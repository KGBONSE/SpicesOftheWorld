### 2 · Geography & Origin
Ethiopia, Somalia, Eritrea — the Horn of Africa. Addis Ababa is home to
Africa's largest spice market. The signature spices here are korarima —
that's Ethiopian or "false" cardamom — fenugreek, chilli pepper, true
cardamom, cumin, and coriander.

*On-screen: "Addis Ababa — Africa's largest spice market" · B-roll: map
graphic, spice market stalls*

### 3 · Trade & Migration History
Fenugreek might be the oldest spice we've covered yet — the earliest seeds
ever found came from an archaeological site in Iraq, dating back to 4000
BCE. They turned up in Tutankhamun's tomb in Egypt too. By Roman times it
was so common it was just cattle fodder — that's actually where the name
comes from, Latin for "Greek hay." A Greek physician called Dioscorides
wrote about it as a spice crop in the 1st century CE. These days it's a
staple across Iran, West Asia, India, Sri Lanka — and it's spread worldwide
through commercial curry powder, where it's a key ingredient.

*On-screen: "Fenugreek seeds found in Tutankhamun's tomb" · B-roll:
historical map/timeline graphic*

### 4 · The Science
Fenugreek's flavour comes down to a compound called sotolon — sweet, like
maple syrup or caramel. There's woody caryophyllene in there too, and if
you toast the seeds before using them, you get these nutty, roasted, almost
coffee-and-chocolate notes from compounds called pyrazines. That's the
move — toast them first.

And fenugreek's one of the better-supported spices in this whole series
health-wise — research genuinely backs it for blood sugar, which is exactly
the kind of everyday benefit worth knowing sits inside something this old.
It's in good company here, too: the turmeric that colours niter kibbeh has
real trial evidence for joint pain, cumin brings its own digestive and
metabolic research, and ginger is about the best-backed spice going for
settling the stomach.

*On-screen: "Sotolon = the maple-syrup note" / "Toast the seeds first" /
"Fenugreek: blood sugar · Turmeric: joint pain · Ginger: digestion" ·
B-roll: seeds toasting in a pan, close-up*

### 5 · The Blend — Niter Kibbeh
This is Ethiopia's spiced clarified butter, and for once I've actually got
real measurements for you. Melt 500 grams of unsalted butter with chopped
onion, garlic, grated ginger, fenugreek seeds, ground cumin, korarima or
cardamom seeds, turmeric, dried oregano, basil, and sage. Simmer it for 20
minutes, then strain it through muslin.

*On-screen: "NITER KIBBEH: 500g butter + onion, garlic, ginger, fenugreek,
cumin, korarima/cardamom, turmeric, oregano, basil, sage — simmer 20 min,
strain" · B-roll: butter melting, spices going in, straining through
muslin*

### 6 · The Dish — Doro Wat
*(Can be voiced over the stew footage.)*

Niter kibbeh isn't a finished dish on its own — it's the base under
Ethiopia's most famous stews. Kitfo, the minced-beef dish, gets it stirred
straight through; misir wat, the red lentil stew, leans on it too. But the
dish it's most identified with is doro wat, Ethiopia's national chicken
stew — so that's the one we're building today.

*On-screen: "Niter kibbeh also builds: kitfo (minced beef) · misir wat
(lentil stew)" then "DORO WAT" · B-roll: stew simmering, niter kibbeh being
stirred in, doro wat plated*
