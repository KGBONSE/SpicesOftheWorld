## 2. Geography & Origin

West Bengal and Bangladesh — the river delta, where cooking is built
around fish and seafood more than almost anywhere else in South Asia. For
religious reasons, many Hindus in Kolkata avoid cooking with onion and
garlic altogether, leaning instead on nutty poppy seed paste, popped
mustard seeds, and ginger to carry a dish. Bangladeshi Muslim cooking
takes a more assertive path — Mughal-style biryanis and beef curries
built on whole spices.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Kolkata — some cooks skip onion and garlic entirely"

## 3. Trade & Migration History

Panch phoran translates simply as "five spices" in Bengali, and it's one
of the few blends in this whole series that stays completely whole from
start to finish — no grinding at any point. Even this one isn't fixed
though: further inland, some versions of the same blend swap out mustard
seed for a local seed called radhuni instead, same five-spice idea, a
different fifth ingredient depending on what's actually growing nearby.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Panch phoran = 'five spices' — the fifth one changes by region"

## 4. The Science

Mustard seed is the backbone of this blend, and it's genuinely one of the
better-researched spices going — its glucosinolates show real
anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial action, tied to lower cardiovascular
risk, and there's a properly useful cooking finding too: using mustard as
a marinade or coating before grilling has been shown to reduce the
harmful compounds that form when meat chars.

## 5. The Blend — Panch Phoran

Simply combine 2 tsp cumin seeds, 2 tsp brown mustard seeds, 2 tsp fennel
seeds, 1 tsp nigella seeds, and 1 tsp fenugreek seeds — no grinding
required. Fry a teaspoonful in oil or ghee at the start of cooking, or
temper a dish with it just before serving.

## 6. The Dish — Masor Tenga Fish Curry

Panch phoran is particularly good with dhals and vegetable dishes, but
the dish that actually shows it off best is a sour fish curry from the
same river delta it comes from.

**[ON-SCREEN TEXT]:** "Panch phoran also lifts: dhals · vegetable dishes"

Rub fish fillets with salt and turmeric, then pan-fry until crisp. In the
same pan, temper panch phoran in hot oil until the seeds crackle and pop,
then soften onion and chillies, add chopped tomatoes, and stir in a
mustard paste. Add water and a good squeeze of lime, bring to a gentle
simmer, then return the fried fish to the pan just to heat through.
Garnish with coriander, serve with rice.

## Africa Link / Throughline Close

What stays with me about panch phoran is that it never gets ground down
into a powder at all — it goes into the pan exactly as it grew, five
whole seeds, and the heat does the rest. That's not so different from how
I think about the chillies I grow on my own farm — sometimes the least
processed version of a spice is the most honest one, and this whole
region built an entire cooking style around trusting that.
