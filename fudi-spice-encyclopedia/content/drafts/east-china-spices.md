Eastern Chinese cooking is a study in contrasts, and the terrain explains most of it. The signature spices are ginger, white pepper, Sichuan pepper and sesame, with star anise, cinnamon and lotus seed in support, and black cardamom, fennel, cumin and liquorice behind them.

## Mountains and coast

Inland, around the Yellow Mountains and Huangshan, Anhui cooking is the preserve of unique foraged ingredients, often sweetened with sugar into hearty peasant food. On the coast, Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Shanghai offer a more refined, gourmet cuisine built on subtly aromatic flavours that show off the region's abundant seafood and river food.

## Lotus seeds

Lotus seeds have a mild, sweet flavour reminiscent of fresh almonds and pine nuts. They are one of the quiet pleasures of this region's cooking.

## A Nanjing spice bag

Put 6 cloves, 4 star anise, 1 teaspoon of crushed whole white pepper, 6 bay leaves, some dried tangerine peel, 1 teaspoon of liquorice root powder and 1 teaspoon of salt into a muslin bag, tied tightly. Drop it into the cooking water with sliced fresh ginger and Shaoxing wine. It is used to flavour the stock for Nanjing's renowned salted duck.
