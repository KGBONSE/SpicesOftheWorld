If I had to describe the Horn of Africa in three words, I would say fiery, aromatic and smoky. It is a part of the world where spice is not an extra, it is the whole point of the pot, and where Addis Ababa is home to the largest spice market on the continent.

## Bittersweet and floral

Ethiopian cooking has a taste you never forget. It comes from bittersweet fenugreek sitting beside korarima, a smoky, floral seed often called Ethiopian or false cardamom. Add chilli, cumin, coriander and cardamom, and you have the base of a whole cuisine, with turmeric, ginger, garlic, nigella and clove playing supporting roles.

Over the border in Somalia the balance shifts. There, the fruitiness of coriander, the clean freshness of cardamom and the earthiness of cumin lead the way. Same corner of the map, entirely different character.

## Two famous blends

Two blends define the region's heat. Berbere is the famous one, and its milder cousin mitmita is just as loved. Both are dominated by red, hot piri piri chillies, which is where all that colour and fire comes from.

## Spiced butter, the quiet hero

If you want to taste the Horn of Africa without any heat at all, make niter kibbeh, a spiced clarified butter. Melt 500g of unsalted butter with chopped onion, garlic and grated ginger, then add fenugreek seeds, ground cumin, korarima or cardamom seeds, turmeric, and a little dried oregano, basil and sage. Let it simmer gently for about 20 minutes, then strain it through muslin. What you get is golden and perfumed, and a spoonful transforms whatever it touches.

## A bridge to somewhere else

Korarima is often compared to true cardamom, the kind grown across South and Southeast Asia. It is a lovely reminder that spices which seem to belong to different worlds often rhyme with one another.
