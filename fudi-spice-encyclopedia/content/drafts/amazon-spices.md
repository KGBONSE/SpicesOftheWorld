The Amazon is the world's largest rainforest, stretching across nine countries, mostly Brazil, Peru and Colombia. Its cooking reflects ancestral traditions, the meeting of those three countries, and the European influence of the Portuguese and Spanish.

## Hundreds of chillies

Black pepper, garlic and cumin are signature flavours, but the real stars are the chillies: ají amarillo, ojito de pez, pinguita de mono, pucunucho, cumari, malagueta and cayenne, among many others. The Amazon holds hundreds of native chilli species in every shape and size, some of them among the hottest in the world. They are often used with garlic, cumin and turmeric to marinate meat and fish.

## Unusual ingredients

Then come the rainforest's stranger gifts: annatto, açaí berries, jambú, tonka beans and turmeric, with priprioca, guaraná, ginger, cinnamon, clove, paprika, vanilla, copoazú and macambo further back.

Jambú is a flower with a numbing effect, which has earned it the nickname "toothache plant". It is used in tacacá, one of Brazil's most popular soups. Guaraná seeds are used more as a stimulant than a flavouring. And copoazú and macambo are both relatives of cacao.

## Tucupí

Tucupí is a Brazilian broth-like sauce served with duck, pork or fish. It is made from cassava, fermented and then simmered with garlic, hot red chillies and black pepper. It is one of those dishes that shows how much knowledge lives in the forest's kitchens.
