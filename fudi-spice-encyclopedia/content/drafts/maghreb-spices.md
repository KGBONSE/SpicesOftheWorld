The Maghreb is a doorway. Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and Libya sit exactly where Africa meets the Middle East and Europe, and you can taste every one of those meetings in the food. Compared with the fire of the Horn or the heat of West Africa, the spicing here is gentle, sweet and rich, more about warmth than burn.

## Cumin and cinnamon

Four spices lead: cumin, cinnamon, ginger and chilli. Cumin is the everyday workhorse, the one that finds its way into almost everything. Cinnamon is the aromatic one, bringing warmth to the fruit-laden tagines that this region is famous for. Behind them come coriander, turmeric, clove, paprika and allspice.

## Layers of history

That list is really a history lesson. The cooking was shaped over centuries by the exchange between the Berbers, the indigenous people of the region, and the rulers who followed: Arabs, Ottomans and the French. There was even a time when Berbers themselves ruled much of the Iberian Peninsula, as Al-Andalus.

Arab traders brought coriander, turmeric and cloves from the east. Paprika and allspice arrived much later, from the Americas, which means this North African kitchen has a thread running all the way across the Atlantic.

## Harissa, from scratch

To taste the Maghreb's fire, make harissa. Soak 100g of dried red chillies (Baklouti if you can find them) in hot water for 30 minutes. Blend them with three or four garlic cloves, salt, lemon juice, ground cumin, ground coriander, ground caraway and olive oil until you have a thick paste. It keeps well and goes with almost anything.
