Korean food has only recently become a global favourite, but it has won hearts quickly. The secret is a heady mix of heat and acidity that is complex and robust, yet somehow subtle.

## Chilli, garlic and sesame

The signature spices are sesame, red chilli and garlic, with ginger, black pepper and perilla seeds supporting, and green chilli and cinnamon in the wings. Korea's neighbours, China and Japan, shaped its cooking, and chilli, ginger and garlic play major roles. But what makes Korean food its own is the craft of fermentation and preservation.

## Balance is everything

The pungency of chilli and garlic is balanced by the rich, toasty flavour of sesame, or mellowed through fermentation, as in kimchi. It is a lesson in how a strong flavour can be tamed without being dulled.

## Yangnyeomjang

This is a deeply flavoured seasoning sauce, dip and marinade. Dry-roast 2 teaspoons of white sesame seeds until golden. Mix them with 3 tablespoons of soy sauce, 1 tablespoon of toasted sesame oil, half a teaspoon of rice vinegar, 2 teaspoons of gochugaru (dried chilli flakes), half a teaspoon of caster sugar or honey, 1 crushed garlic clove and 1 sliced spring onion. It keeps in the fridge for up to a week.
