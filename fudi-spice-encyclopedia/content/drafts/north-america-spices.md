North American cooking started with a very small spice cupboard. European settlers brought only a limited range of spices, mainly to preserve food on the long Atlantic crossing. Over time, flavours from Central America (chilli, annatto), the Caribbean (allspice) and South America (molle) left their mark, though only chilli really stuck, thanks to Mexico being so close.

## Hot, sweet, eclectic

Chilli leads the way today: jalapeño, poblano, ancho and cayenne, with garlic, black and white pepper and vanilla. Behind them come paprika, mustard, ginger, nutmeg, cinnamon and cloves, and then anise, sesame, cumin, turmeric and mace. After garlic and chillies, pepper and vanilla are the most common imports, bringing everyday warmth to savoury and sweet dishes. Table mustards tend to be mild. Nutmeg, cinnamon and cloves are the spices of choice for sweet baking.

## A real fusion

What makes North American food special is how easily it welcomes everyone. Mexican fajitas, Indian curries and Japanese ramen all sit comfortably in the everyday diet.

## A barbecue rub

Mix 1 tablespoon of ground cumin, half a tablespoon of cayenne pepper, 1 tablespoon of chilli powder, 3 tablespoons of brown sugar (a Kansas City touch), 1 tablespoon of ground black pepper, 2 tablespoons of smoked paprika (from the Memphis rub tradition), 1 tablespoon of onion powder, 2 tablespoons of sea salt and 1 tablespoon of garlic powder. Use it as a dry rub on steak, brisket, pulled pork or chicken wings.
