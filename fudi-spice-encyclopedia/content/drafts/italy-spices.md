Italy played a key role in the story of spice, when the Republic of Venice dominated the trade from the 8th to the 15th centuries. Today's regional palettes reflect Italy's past as a collection of separate states. The signature spices are chilli (peperoncino), black pepper and fennel, with coriander, nutmeg, garlic, saffron, clove and vanilla in support.

## Everywhere, a little

Fennel, nutmeg and saffron are popular nationwide, in meats, cheeses, pasta and risottos. More than anywhere else in Europe, black pepper in Italy is more than a mere seasoning. Almost every Italian cheese dish seems to feature nutmeg. In the fiery food of Calabria, hot peperoncino chilli spices cheese, salami and oil, and Calabrians pair liquorice with game and use it to flavour liqueurs.

## Sweet and savoury

Cloves feature in both sweet and rustic savoury dishes, such as the compact Sienese tart panforte. Cacao nibs may appear in the sweet-and-sour agrodolce sauce.

## Arrabiata

"Arrabiata" means "angry", and the sauce earns its name from liberal chilli and black pepper. Combine 1 tablespoon of hot red chilli flakes, 1 teaspoon of dried oregano, 1 teaspoon of garlic powder, 1 teaspoon of salt and 1 teaspoon of ground black pepper. Heat 1 tablespoon of olive oil and fry 50g of cubed pancetta with 1 finely chopped onion for 5 minutes. Add the spice mix and 2 cans (400g each) of chopped tomatoes, then simmer for 10 minutes.
