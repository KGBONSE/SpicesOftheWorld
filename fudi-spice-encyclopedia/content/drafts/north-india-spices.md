North Indian cooking spans everything from no-frills Punjabi home cooking to the refined kitchens of the Mughal palaces. The kebabs and hearty dhals, finished with sizzling cumin at roadside cafés, have travelled the world as widely as the cuisine itself.

## The foundation

Five spices sit at the base of the essential masala mixes and pastes: cumin, turmeric, chilli, ginger and garlic. Cassia, clove, cardamom, Indian bay and fennel support them. Indian bay is in the same plant family as cinnamon, and tastes like a mix of clove, cinnamon and allspice. Black cumin, anardana (dried pomegranate seed), peppercorns and saffron are on the supplementary shelf.

## Royal blends, home warmth

During the era of the Maharajas and Nawabs, royal chefs built flamboyant spice blends for elaborate dishes, some with a dozen or more aromatics. Home cooks today tend to favour the warming character of garam masala over raw chilli heat. Garam masala has its own article on this site.

## Garam masala

Grind 25g of black cardamom seeds, 25g of cassia (broken into pieces), 25g of black peppercorns, 10g of black cumin seeds, 2 blades of mace, 10g of cloves and an eighth of a chopped nutmeg to a fine powder, sieving out any fibres. Many households have their own variation of this warming blend.
