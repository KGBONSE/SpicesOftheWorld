Spice has been part of the food of Iraq since the rulers of ancient Mesopotamia, and you can taste that long memory in the cooking. It is sweet, fragrant and sour all at once, with black pepper, cardamom, cinnamon and cumin at its core.

## Layers of the trade routes

Each wave of trade left something behind. Persian traders added fresh, fruity spices. Then the Silk Road and Spice Route brought sweet-and-sour Chinese cassia alongside cardamom, turmeric and fenugreek from South Asia. Allspice, garlic, tamarind, saffron and dried lime joined the family, with coriander, paprika and ginger on the edges.

## Pepper for lamb, sour for fish

Black pepper is the favourite seasoning for lamb, Iraq's best-loved meat. Sourness has its own place: tamarind and turmeric give the famous grilled carp dish, masgouf, its character.

## A blend made for your family

Something I find lovely: spices are so central to Iraqi home cooking that families traditionally have their own baharat made for them at the local market. Your household's blend is yours alone.

## An Arabic baharat

Grind 1 tablespoon of black peppercorns, 1 tablespoon of allspice, 1 teaspoon of cloves, 1 teaspoon of coriander seeds and 1 teaspoon of cumin seeds. Combine with 1 tablespoon of cinnamon and 1 teaspoon of freshly grated nutmeg. Use it as an all-purpose rub, marinade or seasoning.
