Turkey has always been a place where things change hands. Sitting where Europe, Asia and the Middle East meet, it has been a spice-trading hub for centuries, and its food tastes fresh, mild and a little smoky as a result.

## The Spice Bazaar

Picture the domed Spice Bazaar in Istanbul. Historically it held Iranian saffron and Sichuan pepper side by side with homegrown treasures like pul biber and urfa biber. Pul biber is a mild, coarsely ground chilli. Urfa biber is darker and smokier. Together with black pepper, cumin, garlic and sumac, they make up the heart of the Turkish spice rack, with paprika, allspice, nigella and cinnamon in support.

## Two Turkeys on one plate

The coast and the east cook quite differently. Along the Aegean and Mediterranean, dishes lean on fresh herbs and lighter spicing. Travel east and the spicing grows heavier and sweeter, closer to the flavours of the wider Middle East. Asian flavours turn up too, fenugreek, clove, coriander and cardamom among them, a reminder of how far the trade routes once reached.

## A Turkish baharat

Grind 2 tablespoons of black peppercorns, 2 tablespoons of cumin seeds, 2 tablespoons of coriander seeds, 1 teaspoon of cloves and half a tablespoon of cardamom seeds. Mix in 1 tablespoon of ground nutmeg and a pinch of cinnamon, then stir in 1 tablespoon of dried mint. It makes about 9 tablespoons, and it is wonderful in kofte, pilafs and roasted vegetables, especially aubergine.
