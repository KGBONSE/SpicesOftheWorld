Indonesia is spread across an archipelago of more than 17,000 islands, and its spice palette is as eclectic as its people's heritage. Indigenous nutmeg, mace and cloves mingle with fresh aromatic leaves and imports from Persia, India and China to create genuinely complex dishes. Its Moluccan islands were the Spice Islands that other nations schemed to control, a story we touch on in the France article.

## If your eyes do not water

A traditional saying goes: "if your eyes do not water, the food is not good." It reflects how thoroughly the nation embraced the chilli after it arrived in the 16th century, though there is far more to Indonesian food than heat.

## The spice rack

The signature spices are garlic, ginger and shallot. Supporting them are nutmeg, mace, clove, salam leaf, galangal, lemongrass, pandan leaf, lime leaf, tamarind, chilli, turmeric, cumin and cinnamon, with black pepper, coriander, curry leaf, cardamom, curry powder, caraway and anise behind.

## Bumbu

Nearly every Indonesian recipe is built on some version of this base spice paste, and every region and every cook has a favourite. Pound 1 teaspoon of coriander seeds, 3 cloves, half a teaspoon of white peppercorns and a pinch of grated nutmeg with 1.5cm of galangal and 2.5cm of fresh turmeric. Add 6 chopped shallots, 3 chopped garlic cloves and 2 chopped red chillies, and finally mix in 1 teaspoon of shrimp paste. Fry in 1 tablespoon of oil over high heat, stirring, for about 5 minutes until golden.
