Myanmar's terrain, climate and people are enormously varied, with influences from over 100 indigenous groups, and its food is eclectic, punchy, fragrant and savoury. The signature spices are ginger, garlic and chilli, with garam masala, mild curry powder, sesame, coriander, lemongrass, turmeric and cumin in support, and black pepper, star anise, cinnamon, curry leaf, tamarind and poppy behind.

## A lighter touch than its neighbours

Dry spices from India and Bangladesh are used sparingly, to enhance freshly pounded spice pastes. The cuisine is less sweet than neighbouring Thailand, with sour notes and a preference for subtle pungency over pure heat. Fresh, fragrant leaves, both cultivated and wild, find their way into most dishes, alongside fermented fish products. Pandan leaves add a vanilla-like flavour, especially to coconut-based sweet dishes.

## The Bay of Bengal

Centuries of trade across the Bay of Bengal brought Indian flavours deep into local recipes. Garam masala is a particularly popular import.

## A Burmese garam masala

Dry-roast 1 teaspoon of coriander seeds, 1 teaspoon of black peppercorns, 1 teaspoon of cumin seeds, 2 dried bay leaves, 1 teaspoon of cardamom pods, 1 teaspoon of cloves, a 2.5cm cinnamon stick and 2 star anise until fragrant. Cool, then grind to a fine powder. It is India's most famous spice mix, equally popular here.
