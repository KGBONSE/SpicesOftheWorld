If any Chinese cuisine has travelled the world, it is Cantonese food from Guangdong, the most southerly of China's eight great food traditions. Many people emigrated from this coastal region, and they carried techniques like steaming and stir-frying with them wherever they went.

## Simple and sharp

Cantonese cooking is dominated by simple condiments: light soy sauce, garlic, ginger and sesame oil. The signature spices are garlic, ginger, chilli and sesame, with black pepper, shajiang (sand ginger) and five-spice powder in support, and white pepper and liquorice behind. Shajiang has the same fresh heat as ginger, but with a mildly medicinal edge.

## More intense neighbours

Other southern kitchens, in Hunan, Fujian and Taiwan, lean on more intense flavours through drying, preserving and a wider spice palette. In Taiwan, white pepper is often paired with pork belly.

## Five-spice powder

Grind 2 star anise, 1 teaspoon of fennel seeds, 1 teaspoon of cloves, a 5cm cinnamon stick and 1 tablespoon of whole Sichuan pepper to a fine powder, sieving out any fibres. Use it as a rub for roasting meats, or add it to stir-fries and stews. It is genuinely better homemade than store-bought.
