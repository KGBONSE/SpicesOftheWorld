From Kerala's cardamom hills to Sri Lanka's cinnamon plantations and Andhra Pradesh's famous chilli harvest, the food of this region was shaped by centuries of spice trading. Portuguese, French, Dutch, Southeast Asian and British influences were all adapted to local tastes.

## The spice rack

The signature spices are tamarind, mustard, fenugreek, cumin, cardamom, ginger, garlic, curry leaf and chilli, with cassia, cinnamon, black pepper, nutmeg and asafoetida in support, and sesame, star anise and mace behind. The true stalwarts are sambhar, tart lentils with vegetables, rice in every form, and sour fish masalas. Dishes range from mild and mellow to bold and fiery depending on whether the spices are dry-roasted, fried, pounded or left whole.

## Sourness and a Chinese link

Kokum, a dried rind from a fruit in the mangosteen family, is used like tamarind to add sourness. Star anise was most likely introduced to this region by Chinese traders, since very little is actually grown in South Asia, a lovely link towards East Asia.

## Gunpowder

Dry-roast 25g of chana dal and 25g of split urad dal separately until coloured, and toast 25g of fresh or desiccated coconut. Add 1 tablespoon of black sesame seeds, 2 tablespoons of curry leaves and 6 to 8 red chillies, cooking until the chillies darken. Stir in 1 tablespoon of tamarind pulp and a quarter teaspoon of asafoetida and toast for 1 minute, then add 2 teaspoons of jaggery off the heat. Combine everything with the lentils, cool, and grind to a coarse powder. Sprinkle it over rice, dosa and idli, or mix it with oil as a dip.
