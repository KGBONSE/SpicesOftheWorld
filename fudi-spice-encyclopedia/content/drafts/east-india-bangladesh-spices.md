In the river delta of West Bengal and Bangladesh, cooks favour complex spice blends that work especially well with fish and seafood. The signature spices are chilli, mustard, turmeric, cumin, ginger and garlic, with poppy seed, black cardamom and fennel supporting, and coriander, fenugreek and nigella behind.

## Without onion and garlic

For religious reasons, many Hindus in Kolkata avoid cooking with onion and garlic. Instead they lean on nutty-tasting poppy seed paste, popped mustard seeds, ginger and the aromatic five-spice blend panch phoran. Bangladeshi Muslim cooking favours more assertive seasoning, with Mughal-style biryanis and beef curries infused with whole spices.

## Panch phoran

Combine 2 teaspoons of cumin seeds, 2 teaspoons of brown mustard seeds, 2 teaspoons of fennel seeds, 1 teaspoon of nigella seeds and 1 teaspoon of fenugreek seeds. There is no grinding at all. Fry a teaspoonful in oil or ghee at the start of cooking, or temper a dish with it just before serving. It is particularly good with dhals and vegetable dishes.
