Cooking in Nepal, Bhutan and the Tibetan regions is shaped by what the foothills provide, alongside frugal high-altitude staples. Wholesome pulses are common, often finished with a tarka of warming fried cumin, chillies and ginger.

## Chilli as a main ingredient

The signature spices are fenugreek, chilli, timur (a type of Sichuan pepper), ginger and garlic, with black cardamom, asafoetida and turmeric in support, and coriander, ajwain, clove and cumin behind. In Bhutan, chilli is lifted from a seasoning to a main ingredient. Ginger-spiced steamed dumplings and chilli-laden Tibetan broths sit beside Punjabi staples like parathas and naan.

## Little details

Black cumin, shahi jeera, comes from a different but related plant to ordinary cumin and has a slightly smoky flavour. The bitterness of fenugreek is deliberately set against the nuttiness of lentils and pulses. And timur brings a citrusy, astringent note to Nepali masalas.

## Timur ko chhop

This is described as the Himalayan equivalent of salt and pepper. Dry-roast 1 teaspoon of timur (or Sichuan pepper) in a dry pan over low heat until fragrant. Mix it with 2 tablespoons of red chilli powder and 1 teaspoon of salt, then grind to a powder. Use it on soups, as a dip for fried meats, or as a pick-me-up over fried potatoes, noodles and curries.
