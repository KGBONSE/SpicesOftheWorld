Vietnam was less shaped by the spice trade than Malaysia or Indonesia, but it still keeps a well-stocked spice cupboard. Most dishes carry vital base notes from dried aromatics, though it is the abundance of fresh herbs that stands out on the plate. The signature spices are ginger, garlic and chilli, with lemongrass, coriander root, star anise, allspice, turmeric, dill, pandan leaf and black pepper in support, and cardamom, liquorice, fennel, cinnamon and clove behind.

## North, south and the French

Southern Vietnamese cooking is more richly spiced than the north, and sour and salty notes run throughout the country. French colonists later added a delicate hand with more European spices, dill, fennel and liquorice. Bird's eye chillies, popular throughout Vietnam, were most likely introduced by the Portuguese in the 16th century.

## Nuoc cham

No Vietnamese table is complete without a bowl of this dipping sauce. Pound 2 chopped bird's eye chillies, 1 chopped garlic clove and 2 teaspoons of palm sugar to a paste in a pestle and mortar. Squeeze in the juice of half a lime, then add 2 tablespoons of water and 2 tablespoons of Vietnamese fish sauce (nuoc mam). Mix well.
