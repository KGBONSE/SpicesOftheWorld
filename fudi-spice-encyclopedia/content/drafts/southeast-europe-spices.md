The food of the eastern Mediterranean and the former communist states of Southeast Europe, the Balkans, Greece, Romania, Hungary, Georgia and Cyprus, is far from spice-heavy. Greek food in particular favours herbal flavouring. The signature spices are blue fenugreek, black pepper and paprika, with cinnamon, caraway, fennel and chilli in support, and dill, carob, vanilla and mastic behind.

## Two distinctive flavours

Hungarian paprika, introduced by the Turks in the 17th century, and Georgian blue fenugreek represent two of Europe's most distinctive flavours. Blue fenugreek is used almost exclusively in Georgian cuisine, and tastes a little like hay and burnt sugar.

## Warmth without the burn

Chillies appear often in Balkan cooking but rarely for heat. What warmth there is usually comes from black pepper instead. Caraway is often paired with cabbage in Hungary and occasionally with fish in the Balkans. Cinnamon is popular in Romania. Cypriot food is often laced with coriander seeds and, unusually, pine-like mastic.

## Sweet things

Carob is a popular chocolate alternative, used in Greek carob cake, and Hungarian creams and cakes are often infused with vanilla.

## Khmeli-suneli

This is Georgia's universal blend, used as a meat rub, in stews, or sprinkled on almost anything. Grind 1 tablespoon of coriander seeds, 1 teaspoon of black peppercorns and 2 dried bay leaves with 1 tablespoon of blue fenugreek leaves and 1 tablespoon of blue fenugreek seeds. Combine with 1 teaspoon of garlic powder, half a teaspoon of chilli powder, 2 tablespoons each of dried marigold, dried savory and dried marjoram, 1 tablespoon each of dried mint and dried dill, and 1 teaspoon of dried hyssop.
