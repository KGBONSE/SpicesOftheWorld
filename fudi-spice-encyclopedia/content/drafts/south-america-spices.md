From the Gulf of Panama to the tip of Patagonia, South America's Pacific coastline is one long invitation to eat seafood: fried, grilled or stewed. The cooking is fresh, spicy and bright with citrus, and chillies sit at the centre of it.

## Fresh chillies, fresh limes

The signature chillies are ají limo, panca and chombo, with garlic and coriander stalks. Cumin, chilli powder, ginger, fresh lime and turmeric support them, with annatto, sesame and tamarind further back. Ají limo is a hot chilli with a strong lemony flavour, named after the Lima region, and chillies are used fresh in ceviche.

## Many hands in the pot

Indigenous culture, Spanish colonisation, the slave trade, and Asian and Arab migrations have all left their mark. You can taste that range in everything from delicate chilli-and-coconut fish soups to hearty charquicán, a beef and vegetable stew.

## A Japanese connection

Look closely and you will find Nikkei cooking, a fusion of Japanese and Peruvian food that draws on ginger, sesame, tamarind and umeboshi, pickled plum paste. It is a lovely echo of the East Asia stories on this site.

## Leche de tigre

This is the marinade for ceviche, traditionally drunk after the fish has been eaten. Combine half to one finely chopped ají limo (or habanero), 1 crushed garlic clove, 2.5cm of coarsely grated ginger, 1 tablespoon of finely chopped fresh coriander stalks, half a finely chopped small red onion and the juice of 5 limes. Season with salt and refrigerate for an hour. It is sharp, fiery and startlingly good.
