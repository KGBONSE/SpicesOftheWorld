Iberian cooking is smoky, sweet and piquant. The signature flavours are pimentón, which is Spanish paprika, and chilli, with dried nora peppers, piri piri, garlic and saffron supporting, and choricero peppers, guindilla and alegrías chillies, cacao, anise, cloves and cinnamon behind.

## Peppers from the new world

Sweet and chilli peppers were brought back from the Americas by Columbus in the late 1400s, and they are now inescapable here. Spanish cooks often reach for the mildness of pimentón, while the Portuguese favour the heat of malagueta chillies. Spain's vivid red pimentón comes in three varieties: dulce (sweet), agridulce (bittersweet) and ahumado (smoked).

## Everyday treasures

Dried nora peppers are essential to the Catalan fish sauce romesco. Green guindilla chillies flavour a hot sauce from the Canary Islands. Cinnamon laces sweet Portuguese dishes. Cacao nibs occasionally season game and beef stews, and anise is paired with chestnuts in Portugal and dried figs in Catalonia.

## Saffron

Saffron abounds in Andalusian cooking and in the Valencian classic, paella. Arab conquerors from North Africa first introduced saffron cultivation to the Iberian Peninsula in the 8th century, a lovely link back to the Middle East stories on this site.

## A paella mix

Grind 2 pinches of saffron threads with a pestle and mortar. Combine with 3 tablespoons of pimentón (a mix of smoked and sweet), 2 teaspoons of garlic powder, 2 teaspoons of onion powder, 1 teaspoon of cayenne pepper, 1 teaspoon of ground black pepper, 1 teaspoon of dried oregano, half a teaspoon of dried parsley, half a teaspoon of ground cloves and half a teaspoon of ground cumin. It makes about 6 tablespoons.
