The Andes run for some 4,500 miles, from Argentina and Chile up through Bolivia, Peru and Ecuador to Colombia and Venezuela. Up here, at a thousand metres and far above, people have always cooked slowly. Hearty stews and soups warm the table, and chillies do most of the talking.

## The yellow chilli

The signature chillies are ají amarillo, rocoto (also called locoto) and merquén, with cumin and garlic beside them. Ají amarillo, the yellow chilli, has been cultivated for more than 8,000 years and was a key ingredient in Inca cooking. Alongside come white and black pepper, molle, turmeric, cinnamon and paprika, sweet and smoked, with annatto, coriander and mustard further back.

## Pink berries from a peppertree

Molle are the dried pink berries of the Peruvian peppertree. They look and taste a little like peppercorns, though they come from an entirely unrelated plant.

## Sauces for the table

Salsas are everywhere. In Peru, uchucuta is a spicy salsa of rocoto chilli and herbs. Its Bolivian and Argentine cousin is llajua.

## Chimichurri

Chimichurri is a fresh sauce served widely with sausages and steak. Combine 1 teaspoon of chilli powder, half a teaspoon of dried chilli flakes, half a teaspoon of paprika, 2 finely chopped garlic cloves, 1 finely chopped onion, 4 teaspoons of finely chopped parsley or coriander, 1 teaspoon of finely chopped oregano, 3 teaspoons of white or red wine vinegar and 6 tablespoons of olive oil. Season with salt and black pepper. Spoon it over anything that has been near a fire.
