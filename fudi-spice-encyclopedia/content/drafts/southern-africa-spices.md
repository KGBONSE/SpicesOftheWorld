Southern African cooking is hot, sweet and wonderfully diverse, and that diversity has a clear cause. In South Africa especially, you can trace almost every layer of the food back to a chapter of history.

## One cuisine, many origins

Cinnamon, turmeric, coriander, chilli and garlic lead the way, with clove, black pepper, cumin, ginger, cardamom and bay behind them. Dutch colonisation brought Indonesian-influenced spicing into Cape Malay dishes. Indian labourers brought over under British rule helped make Durban one of the world's great curry cities, and it is often described as the largest Indian city outside India. In Mozambique, the Portuguese left their mark in piri piri chilli blends.

Further inland, Botswana and Zimbabwe cook with spice far more sparingly. The region is not one flavour, it is several.

## A Durban curry masala

To taste that Indian-African story, toast and grind coriander seeds, cumin seeds, cardamom seeds, fenugreek seeds, a few cloves and a cinnamon stick, then mix in mild chilli powder, a little cayenne and ground ginger. Use it as the base for a Durban-style curry.

## A migration story in a spice jar

What I find most moving about this part of Africa is that the Durban curry tradition is Indian cooking carried across the ocean by people who had to leave home. It is a reminder that spices do not travel alone, they travel with the people who love them.
