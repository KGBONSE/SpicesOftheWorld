Few places have given the world's kitchens as much as Mexico and Central America. Chillies, cacao and vanilla all began their journeys here, and the everyday cooking of the region is as layered as its history.

## A palette of chillies

Chilli is the heart of it all: ancho, jalapeño, pasilla, mulato, chipotle and chile de árbol, with paprika and cumin close behind. Garlic, black pepper, cinnamon, clove and vanilla play supporting roles, and cacao, sesame, allspice, Mexican oregano, coriander and annatto sit further back. There are some 60 native chillies, used fresh, dried whole or ground, each with its own heat and character.

## Layers of history

Belizean food draws on the Maya, the Garifuna, Creole and British traditions. Mexican cooking owes a debt to the Maya, the Aztecs and Spanish colonial cooks. The Maya often used red and green jalapeños. The Aztecs used chipotle chillies, cacao and vanilla. The Spanish conquistadores carried these treasures around the world, and they brought cumin, pepper, cloves and cinnamon with them in return.

## Small, lovely details

Cinnamon traditionally flavours Mexican chocolate bars. Annatto is used mainly to give meats, rice and stews their warm colour.

## A mole mix

Every country has its own mole, always built on three different chillies. Toast 2 each of dried ancho, pasilla and mulato chillies with 3 tablespoons of cacao nibs, 1 tablespoon of sesame seeds, a 2cm cinnamon stick, a quarter teaspoon each of cumin seeds, anise seeds, whole black peppercorns and cloves, a quarter teaspoon each of dried thyme and marjoram, and a crumbled dried bay leaf. Grind it all, then stir it into fried onions and garlic and simmer with chopped tomatoes to make a mole sauce.
