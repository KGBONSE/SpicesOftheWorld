East African cooking tastes zingy, sweet and fragrant, and the reason sits on an island. Zanzibar and the archipelago around it were the great spice crossroads of the region, and the flavours that came through them are still on the table in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda today.

## Ginger leads

Ginger gives the cooking its piquancy and its heat. Alongside it come cardamom, garlic, cinnamon and clove, with bay, black pepper, cumin and coriander in support. The sweeter notes, cardamom, clove and cinnamon, are exactly what you taste in the region's own version of chai.

## Two thousand years of trade

For more than two thousand years, ships carried cloves, ginger, pepper, cinnamon and cardamom between East Africa and Arabia, Persia and India. In the 1500s, under Omani settlers, Zanzibar's clove trade brought the island enormous wealth.

The spices did not stay at the coast. Ginger and cardamom travelled furthest inland, while coastal kitchens leaned on turmeric and coriander, often with coconut.

## A pilau masala to try

Toast whole cumin seeds, black peppercorns, cloves, cardamom seeds and a piece of cinnamon until they smell wonderful, then grind them together. Stir the mix into the pan when you fry the onions for pilau rice. Everything in the pan will smell of the coast.

## A natural bridge

Those same trade routes carried cardamom, clove and pepper in from India, which makes East Africa one of the clearest links between the African spice story and South Asia's. Follow the spices and the continents start to feel a lot closer together.
