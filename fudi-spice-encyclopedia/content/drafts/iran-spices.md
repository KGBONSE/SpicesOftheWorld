Iran sits at the old heart of the Persian Empire and the Silk Road, and its food tastes like a place that has welcomed everyone. Over the centuries Greeks, Arabs, Turks, Mongols and Uzbeks all passed through, and the cooking absorbed something from each. Pistachios, pomegranates and walnuts share the table with smoked fish, hot-and-sour prawns and even Chinese noodles.

## Saffron, roses and sumac

Three spices lead: saffron, sumac and rose. Behind them come dried lime, barberry, angelica, cinnamon, cumin, ginger, turmeric and garlic. Stews and pilafs lean on sweet-and-sour flavours, from sumac, dried limes, barberries and tamarind, while rose petals bring a delicate, perfumed note to spice mixes and sweets.

## The saffron country

Iran produces over 90% of the world's saffron, the most expensive spice there is. And the way it is used matters. Iranian cooks use it as whole, burnt-red strands, never as a powder. It gives seafood and poultry a rich, slightly bitter flavour and turns rice a glowing amber.

## Advieh

Grind 2 tablespoons of dried rose petals, 2 tablespoons of cardamom seeds and 1 tablespoon of cumin seeds. Combine with 2 tablespoons of ground cinnamon and 2 tablespoons of ground ginger. Sprinkle it over savoury rice, rub it into meats, add it to stews, or use it on Persian rice pudding.
