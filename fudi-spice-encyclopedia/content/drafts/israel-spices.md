Israeli food is a conversation between many places. It shares much of its menu with neighbouring Lebanon, and then keeps adding to it, drawing on flavours that people carried here from all over the world.

Which brings us to hummus, the dish both countries claim with equal passion. Its origin is hotly disputed between Israel and Lebanon, and we tell the full, friendly argument in the Lebanon story. Think of it as the Middle East's version of the jollof debate.

## Spices that travelled in

The foundation is sesame, sumac, cumin, garlic and paprika, with caraway, chilli, fenugreek, nigella, black pepper, cinnamon, cardamom and coriander behind them. On top of that sit influences from far away: fiery chilli pastes from Yemen, paprika from Russia and Eastern Europe, harissa from North Africa, and, more and more, Korean, Thai and Mexican flavours brought home by travellers. It is a palette that never stops growing.

## Small details that say a lot

Caraway seasons Jewish rye bread. Nigella seeds scatter over pittas. And dried hibiscus, steeped in water or ground to a powder, adds a sharp, cranberry-like fruitiness that you rarely find elsewhere in the Middle East but see all the time here.

## Zhug

Zhug is the bright green chilli condiment of the national table, served with falafel, grilled meats and eggs. Roast and grind 1 teaspoon each of cardamom, coriander and cumin seeds. Pound them to a paste with 2 chopped green chillies and 2 chopped garlic cloves. Mix in a large bunch each of chopped coriander and flat-leaf parsley, and 2 tablespoons of olive oil. Season with salt, pepper and the juice of half a lemon. It is fresh, fierce and addictive.
