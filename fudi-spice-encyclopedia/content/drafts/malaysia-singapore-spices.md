The southern Malay port of Melaka was perfectly placed to become a major trading hub, both for regional shipping and for spice-route traffic heading further afield. The result is that significant Chinese, Indian and Muslim communities still live in Malaysia and Singapore today, and the cuisine is defined by its willingness to embrace different cultures.

## The birthplace of fusion

Fusion food as a concept was arguably born in Melaka, where Chinese, Indian, Arabic and European ingredients regularly ended up in the same pot. The signature spices are ginger, garlic and chilli. Supporting them are cardamom, turmeric, dried chilli, black pepper, coriander seed and root, cumin, cinnamon, curry leaf, lemongrass, galangal, tamarind, pandan leaf and lime leaf, with fennel, anise, star anise, dried ginger, cassia, Sichuan pepper and sesame behind.

## A Malaysian fish curry paste

Toast 2 teaspoons of coriander seeds, 1 teaspoon of cumin seeds, half a teaspoon of fennel seeds, half a teaspoon of black peppercorns and 4 dried chillies. Cool, then grind with half a teaspoon of turmeric. Separately, blend a thumb-sized piece of galangal, 3 garlic cloves and 50g of shallots with a splash of water into a purée. Combine the dry masala with the fresh purée to form the curry paste, then simmer it with coconut milk and firm white fish.
