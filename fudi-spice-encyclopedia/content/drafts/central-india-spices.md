Central India borrows from its neighbours, Rajasthan, Gujarat and Maharashtra, and turns vegetarian staples into standout dishes through contrasting combinations of spice. The signature spices are coriander, cumin, dried red chillies, turmeric and ginger, with asafoetida, fennel and nigella in support, and ajwain and amchoor, dried mango powder, behind.

## A simple trick

A well-known seasoning here is a trio of fried nigella, fenugreek and fennel seeds. It gives a pickled edge to softened vegetables and meaty masalas. In Bhopal, cafés serve biryanis, kebabs and aromatic curries built on garam masala, boosted with plenty of pounded coriander seed.

## Five spices, different countries

Panch phoran means "five spices". Bengali versions of the blend swap in the local radhuni seed for mustard seed, a small detail that says a lot about how a single idea changes from place to place.

## Chaat masala

Dry-roast 2 tablespoons of cumin seeds, 1 teaspoon of coriander seeds and 1 tablespoon of black peppercorns until fragrant. Cool them, then mix with 3 tablespoons of amchoor, 1 teaspoon of ground ginger, half a teaspoon of asafoetida, 1 tablespoon of kala namak (Himalayan black salt) and 1 teaspoon of salt. Grind to a powder. Sprinkle it over street snacks, fruit salads and kebabs.
