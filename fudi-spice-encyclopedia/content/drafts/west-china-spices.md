Western China is where the Silk Road left its mark. Travellers from western and central Asia brought unfamiliar spices, and the result is a wide range of sensations: sour, sweet, bitter, salty, hot and spicy, especially in far-western Xinjiang.

## Sichuan pepper and chilli

The signature spices are bird's eye chilli, Sichuan pepper and garlic, with star anise, ginger, sesame and black cardamom in support, and cumin, saffron and cinnamon behind. Western Chinese cooking has been dominated by Sichuan pepper for more than 2,000 years. Its tingling, numbing story has an article of its own on this site.

## Xinjiang

In Xinjiang, more than half the population is Muslim, and spices like cumin and saffron, along with dried fruits, feature heavily in Uyghur and Kazakh dishes of lamb, chicken and vegetables. In Kashgar's market, flavours that made their way here from the Middle East, cumin, cinnamon and saffron, are still sold today.

## A chilli black bean sauce

This is a homemade version of doubanjiang, the Sichuan fermented bean paste. Fry 200g of finely chopped red bird's eye chillies and 50g of rinsed, chopped fermented black beans in 2 tablespoons of vegetable oil for 1 minute. Add 1 tablespoon of rice wine and 2 teaspoons of dark rice vinegar and simmer for 3 minutes. Add 1 tablespoon of sugar and simmer for 10 minutes. Add 3 garlic cloves and a 7.5cm piece of chopped ginger and cook until the oil floats to the surface. It keeps in the fridge for up to 3 weeks.
