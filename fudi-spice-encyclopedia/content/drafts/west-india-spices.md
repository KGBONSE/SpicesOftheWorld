Western India covers very different landscapes, from Rajasthan's arid climate to the tropical air of Maharashtra and Goa, and the cooking varies with them. The signature spices are kokum, dried red chilli, cumin, turmeric, mustard, ginger and garlic, though garlic is shunned by the Jain community for religious reasons. Fenugreek, curry leaf and black pepper support them, with asafoetida and sesame behind.

## Four kitchens

Gujarati savoury dishes balance sweet and sour with jaggery, set against pungent fenugreek seed and dried red chilli. Royal Rajasthani dishes are known for rich, chilli-laden masalas. Along the Konkan coast, hot-and-sour fish curries are balanced by creamy coconut masalas. And Goa is famous for its vinegary, garlicky Indo-Portuguese dishes.

## Vindaloo paste

Goa adopted and adapted this fiery, sweet-sour mix directly from the Portuguese. Toast 1½ teaspoons of cumin seeds, 15 to 20 whole dried Kashmiri chillies, 1 teaspoon of black peppercorns, 2 teaspoons of black mustard seeds, 1 teaspoon of fenugreek seeds, 6 cloves, 1 teaspoon of cardamom seeds, a 5cm piece of cassia and half a star anise in a dry pan until fragrant. Grind them, then mix with 6 tablespoons of cider or palm vinegar, 1 teaspoon of salt and 2 teaspoons of jaggery (or date palm sugar) into a stiff paste.
