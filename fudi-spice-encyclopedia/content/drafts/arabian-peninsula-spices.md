Travel down the Arabian Peninsula, from Saudi Arabia towards Yemen and Oman, and the food gets hotter, deeper and more complex with every step. It starts rich, dry and musky, and ends fiery.

## The shared foundation

Baharat, dried lime and bay are the signature flavours, with saffron, cardamom, clove, cinnamon, cumin, coriander, garlic, chilli and black pepper close by. The Levantine trio of cumin, coriander and cinnamon is here, just as it is further north, along with dried limes and saffron from Iran.

## Where Africa and Asia arrive

As the peninsula stretches towards Yemen and Oman, with Africa and Asia just beyond, new flavours come in. Cloves, black pepper, masala mixes, turmeric and coconut arrive from Asia, and hot chillies arrive from Africa. Omani and Yemeni cooking are famous for their fiery, heady spice blends, and you can taste a whole sea of trade in a single pot.

## Hawaij

Hawaij is a Yemeni blend, curry-like and warming. Grind 1 tablespoon of black peppercorns, 7 teaspoons of cumin seeds, 1 tablespoon of cardamom seeds and 1 tablespoon of coriander seeds. Combine with 2 tablespoons of ground turmeric. Use it in slow-cooked meats and soups.

There is a sweeter version too. Swap the pepper and cumin for ginger, cloves, cinnamon and fennel, and stir it into coffee and desserts.
