No country along the spice route embraced the chilli as enthusiastically as Thailand. It is impossible to imagine Thai food without it. The signature spices of this part of the world are chilli, garlic, coriander root and lemongrass, with lime leaf, galangal, ginger, krachai (wild ginger), black pepper and tamarind in support.

## Pounded fresh

Like landlocked Laos, Thai cooks still favour freshly pounded spice pastes over dried spice mixes. Cambodia is the gentle one. Its kitchen is blander, and gives a hint of what Thai food might have tasted like before chillies arrived. Its subtler heat comes from black pepper, which has been grown in the country since the 13th century.

## The supplementary shelf

Behind all of this sit turmeric, clove, nutmeg, cinnamon, fennel, cardamom, pandan leaf and green pepper.

## Khao kua

This toasted rice powder is a key ingredient in Laotian larb and Thai beef salad, and its texture can't be substituted. Dry-roast 5 tablespoons of uncooked glutinous rice with 1 sliced lemongrass stalk, a thumb-sized piece of sliced galangal and 3 roughly torn makrut lime leaves in a frying pan over medium heat, stirring frequently, until the rice turns golden brown. Cool it, pick out the aromatics, and pound the rice with a pestle and mortar until coarsely ground.
