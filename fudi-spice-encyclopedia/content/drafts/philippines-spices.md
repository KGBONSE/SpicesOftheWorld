Food is central to Filipino life, with five meals a day the norm, yet the spicing is surprisingly restrained. Most classic recipes use no more than two or three spices, and very few would be called genuinely "spicy".

## The holy trinity

Bay, black pepper and garlic are the signature trio of Filipino cuisine, appearing in nearly every savoury recipe, including the unofficial national dish, adobo. Annatto, turmeric, ginger, lemongrass and pandan leaf support them, with chilli powder, paprika and tamarind behind.

## Colour over heat

More than 300 years of Spanish rule left the palate feeling more European than Asian, and the Philippines never took to chilli the way Spain's other trading partners did. Spices are used more for colour, red from annatto and yellow from turmeric, than for heat. Annatto is a New World native, brought over and now grown locally.

## A Spanish name, a Filipino technique

Adobo is itself a Spanish term, applied by colonists to a native Filipino technique of preserving meat with vinegar and spices.

## Adobo marinade

Combine 100ml of coconut vinegar, 50ml of soy sauce, 3 crushed garlic cloves, 1 teaspoon of black peppercorns and 2 bay leaves in a small saucepan. Bring to the boil, then remove from the heat and cool to room temperature. Use it to marinate chicken or pork overnight.
