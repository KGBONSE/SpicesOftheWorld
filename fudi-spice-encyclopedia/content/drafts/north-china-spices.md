China is so vast that its cooking changes from province to province, but in the north the spicing is more unified. Shandong, Henan and Beijing share a gentle touch, built on ginger, sesame and Sichuan pepper, with star anise and garlic supporting, and cumin and chilli on the edges.

## Three northern kitchens

Shandong cooking favours subtle flavours that preserve the main ingredient rather than dominate it, and it is regarded as the primary cuisine among China's eight major regional traditions. Henan is more delicate but harmonious. Beijing draws from both, with a special love of the sour notes of dark vinegar.

## Cumin and chilli, from the Muslim kitchens

Cumin and chilli came into northern cooking through the kitchens of Chinese Muslims. A Beijing favourite is yangrou chuan, lamb skewers coated in cumin and chilli.

## A Shandong spice bag

Put 10 cardamom pods, 2 star anise, half a tablespoon of whole Sichuan pepper, half a tablespoon of cloves, half a tablespoon of ground cumin, a 7.5cm cinnamon stick, a 10 to 12cm piece of liquorice root and some dried orange or tangerine peel into a muslin bag. Tie it and drop it into the cooking water. It is used to infuse the master stock for northern chicken stews in the Dezhou or Daokou style.
