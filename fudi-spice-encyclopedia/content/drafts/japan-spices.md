For a relatively small archipelago, Japan's cooking and spicing are wonderfully diverse. The overall feeling is fresh, mild and bright, but look closely and there are surprises everywhere.

## The Japanese spice rack

The signature spices are sesame, sanshō, which is similar to Sichuan pepper, and ginger. White pepper, chilli, garlic and star anise support them, with clove, allspice, cinnamon and black pepper further back. Perilla seeds, also called shiso, come from a plant in the mint family. They have a nutty, slightly menthol-and-aniseed flavour and are grown mainly in central Japan.

## The "barbarian" cuisine

In the 16th and 17th centuries, a few foreigners were allowed to visit, and a southern style of cooking grew from that contact, sometimes called "barbarian" cuisine. The Portuguese introduced deep-frying, chillies and vinegar. The Chinese brought ramen, gyoza and dumplings. The British brought curry. This Western-influenced style sits alongside washoku, the more subtle, traditional Japanese cooking found across the rest of the country.

## Shichimi-togarashi

Mix 2 tablespoons of finely ground sanshō (or Sichuan pepper), 2 tablespoons of dried orange, yuzu or lemon peel, 4 tablespoons of chilli powder, 2 tablespoons of nori seaweed flakes, 2 teaspoons of black sesame seeds, 2 teaspoons of hemp, flax or chia seeds and 2 teaspoons of garlic powder. Use it as a table condiment, or on curries, grilled meats and noodles.
