For centuries Egypt's ports were a gateway to the spice riches of the East, long before its great trading peak in the Middle Ages. And yet today the Egyptian kitchen is quietly simple, much more restrained than its Levantine neighbours.

## Cumin is home

If one spice defines Egyptian cooking, it is cumin. It is the everyday flavour, in vegetable soups, meat stews and grain dishes like koshari. Cinnamon comes next, then garlic, black pepper, chilli and coriander, with ginger, cloves, bay, cardamom and allspice on the supplementary shelf. Cooks keep things simple, and when they want a little more complexity, they reach for baharat.

## Dukkah

Dukkah is the everyday nut-and-spice condiment, eaten with olive oil and bread, or used to season roasted meats and hummus. Roast 150g of hazelnuts for 10 minutes and 150g of whole almonds for 8 minutes at 200°C (400°F, Gas 6). Dry-fry 1 tablespoon of coriander seeds, 1 tablespoon of cumin seeds and 3 tablespoons of sesame seeds over high heat for 3 minutes. Grind everything together with a pinch of salt. Dip warm bread in olive oil, then in dukkah, and you will understand the appeal at once.
